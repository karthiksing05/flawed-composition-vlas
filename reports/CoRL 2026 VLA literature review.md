# CoRL 2026 puts VLA binding under scrutiny

CoRL 2026 in Austin accepted **687 of 2,094 submissions (about 33%)**, and as of 2026-11-10, with the meeting still running, no main-conference awards had been announced. Its generalist-policy work splits into two families. The first is VLM-plus-flow-expert VLAs: Physical Intelligence's pi0.7, MEM and RL Token, AI2's MolmoAct2 and MolmoBot, and Being-H0.5. The second is "world action models" that denoise future video and actions together. That family includes the NVIDIA DreamZero oral and **GR00T 2, announced 9 November at CoRL**, which drops the dual-system VLM-plus-DiT design of GR00T N1 through N1.7 for a single diffusion transformer. For a theory of compositional generalization, the important finding is that 2026 work has reached the reader's diagnosis on its own. VLAs execute their skills reliably but bind the wrong referent on held-out pairings, because the scene or object identity predicts the instruction. CRAFT, "Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding", MESA, LIBERO-CF and the Grant et al. activation-injection study all report this. Parts of the reader's repair are already in print. AC-VLA masks the wrist view during placement. LiLo-VLA masks non-target objects. VP-VLA switches its prompts on gripper events. ReGuide lifts frozen pi0.5 from 39% to 94.5% on LIBERO compositions without any training. Four things remain unpublished: a partner-count law on pretrained VLAs (Wang et al. show the law only on a small custom policy), a grasp-then-place decomposition of failures in frozen models, hiding identity while keeping geometry, and composing keyed adapters across unseen pairings. Coverage is partial. About 300 of the 687 accepted papers were seen, through a third-party portal. Most numbers come from abstracts or from a summarizing fetch tool. Several of the closest papers have no confirmed venue.

## Generalist policies split into VLAs and world-action models

### Physical Intelligence and AI2 set the open and closed VLA reference points

The standard 2026 recipe is a pretrained VLM backbone, a flow-matching or DiT action expert, and gradient insulation between the two. **pi0.7** (CoRL oral, arXiv 2604.15483) has about 5B parameters: a 4B Gemma3-initialized VLM plus an 860M flow-matching expert. The backbone is trained only on FAST tokens, and expert gradients do not reach it. pi0.7 adds a MEM-style video encoder (up to six history frames) and real-time chunking. It is steered by metadata (speed, quality, mistake labels) under classifier-free guidance, and by subgoal images from a 14B BAGEL-initialized world model ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15483)). Physical Intelligence calls the result "early signs" of compositional generalization. Zero-shot shirt folding reaches 80% success against 80.6% for humans, and unseen tasks land around 60-80%. Most comparisons against pi0.5 and pi0.6 appear only in figures, and a third-party explainer reports only partial progress on an air-fryer instruction until a human coached the robot ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15483); [Physical AI Lab](https://physicalailab.io/en/physical-intelligence-pi07-steerable-vla/)). The weights are not public; Physical Intelligence offers fine-tuning through a beta API that opened 13 October ([pi.website](https://www.pi.website/blog)). Two companion papers come from the same lab. **MEM** (spotlight, 2603.03596) gives pi0.6 video short-term memory and text long-term memory for tasks up to 15 minutes ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03596)). **RL Token** (oral, 2604.23073) trains a small actor-critic on a compact readout of the VLA and reports up to 3x speed-ups on precise insertions after "minutes to a few hours" of practice ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23073)).

**MolmoAct2** (spotlight, 2605.02881) is the strongest fully open option. It grafts a flow-matching expert onto a discrete-token VLM through per-layer KV conditioning, uses the MolmoER embodied-reasoning VLM and the OpenFAST tokenizer, and claims to beat pi0.5 on seven sim and real benchmarks. Its weights, code and data are stated as released ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02881)). **Being-H0.5** (oral, 2601.12993) trains a Mixture-of-Transformers with a "Mixture-of-Flow" action module (shared motor primitives plus embodiment experts) on a 35,000-hour corpus, reports **98.9% on LIBERO and 53.9% on RoboCasa**, and runs one checkpoint on five robots ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12993)). **MolmoBot** (oral, 2603.16861) trains on 1.7M simulated trajectories alone and reports **79.2% zero-shot real pick-and-place against 39.2% for pi0.5** ([portal](https://hongsong-wang.github.io/CoRL2026)). **BitVLA** (spotlight) is a fully 1-bit VLA built on BitNet b1.58 that uses about 11x less memory ([alphaXiv spotlights](https://www.alphaxiv.org/conferences/corl/2026/spotlight)). **RoboTTT** (oral, 2607.15275) applies fast-weight test-time training over an 8K-step context and reports +87% over a single-step-context baseline ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15275)). **EgoScale** (oral, 2602.16710) pretrains on 20,854 hours of action-labeled egocentric video and reports +54% average success on a 22-DoF hand ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16710)).

Several models that the field treats as state of the art were released outside CoRL, and their numbers are self-reported. **Qwen-VLA** (2605.30280) reports LIBERO 97.9%, SimplerEnv-WidowX 73.7% and real ALOHA OOD 76.9% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30280)). LingBot-VLA 2.0, Xiaomi-Robotics-1 (RoboCasa365 57.6%), G0.5 (real fine-tuning 76.7% against pi0.5's 53.3%), Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA and MiniCPM-Robot are known only through an aggregator ([awesome-VLA](https://github.com/ai4s-research/awesome-vision-language-action)). Among closed systems, DeepMind's **Gemini Robotics 2.5** post is dated 10 November and claims 30-100% success gains over baselines across 200 tasks, plus 96% on Mercedes-Benz kitting with Apptronik Apollo 2 ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/)). Gemini Robotics ER 2 shipped through the API on 30 July ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-robotics-er-2/)). Figure's **Helix 02** adds a "System 0" whole-body controller running at 1 kHz under Systems 1 and 2 ([Figure](https://figure.ai/news/helix-02)). Across all of these, the architectural trends are knowledge insulation, memory, metadata and subgoal conditioning, unified action spaces, human-video pretraining and latent world-model branches. None adds explicit role or object structure inside the policy.

### GR00T 2 replaces the dual-system VLA with one video-action transformer

NVIDIA announced **GR00T 2** on 9 November at CoRL, alongside Isaac Lab 3.0, as an "open frontier world action model" ([Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/nvidia-launches-gr00t-2-isaac-lab-3-0-corl/); [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/robotics-simulation-advancements-corl/)). The release is named "GR00T 2", not "N2". At GTC in March, Jensen Huang previewed a "GR00T N2" based on DreamZero and due by the end of the year ([NVIDIA newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-open-model-families-to-power-the-next-wave-of-agentic-physical-and-healthcare-ai)). GR00T 2 is very likely that model, but no source read says so. The GEAR project page links a November 2026 whitepaper (a tech report, not an arXiv paper), which was too large to fetch. It says code is **"Available in December"** and gives no Hugging Face model, GitHub repository or weight license ([GR00T 2 page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2/)). The only artifact released so far is the dataset nvidia/PhysicalAI-Human-1M-100K.

| Version | Policy structure | Key changes | Reported language or generalization numbers |

The change is structural. N1 through N1.7 kept a (mostly) frozen VLM whose embeddings condition a separate action module. GR00T 2 handles vision, language and action inside one transformer over joint video-action tokens, and moves the planner role outside the model to AI agents that call GR00T 2 tools and draw **"visual steering" marks** on images ([Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/nvidia-launches-gr00t-2-isaac-lab-3-0-corl/)). Every result NVIDIA reports concerns new tasks or human-to-robot transfer. On YAM, agent control lifts toy-assembly success to 97-98% from 71-84% without the agent. Visual steering lifts short-horizon YAM success from 40% to 92%, and trash sorting from 0 to 17 of 20. Real-world RL raises cable insertion from 12/20 to 19/20. G1 Turbo improves sim-to-real OOD success from 6.7% to 63.3%, against 40.0% for GR00T 1.7 ([GR00T 2 page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2/)). The 40% zero-shot figure carries a caveat: press reports describe it as "40 of 50 tasks succeeded at least once", which is a different metric. GR00T 2's page compares only against NVIDIA's own models, and no independent evaluation exists. Its research predecessor, DreamZero (CoRL oral, 2602.15922), reports 62.2% task progress on seen AgiBot tasks against 27.4% for the best pretrained VLA, and **49% on unseen DROID verbs against 25-32% for VLAs** ([DreamZero](https://dreamzero0.github.io)).

Two points follow for the reader. The reader's frozen-GR00T measurements, and most third-party GR00T numbers (MESA with N1.6; CRAFT, ReGuide and Qi et al. with N1.7), concern the dual-system line. That line has a clear seam where identity can be withheld from the action module. GR00T 2 has no such seam, so withholding identity would have to happen at the input, where the reader's silhouette edit already works. Its agent-plus-marks interface also moves referent binding outside the motor model, which is the reader's thesis applied at system level. No source tests GR00T 2 on held-out object-target pairings.

## Data, RL and world models carry the learning-paradigm story

World-action models are the conference's main learning story. Besides DreamZero, **GRAIL** (oral, 2606.05160) generates humanoid loco-manipulation demonstrations from world models grounded in 3D scenes. **SuperMimic** (oral) claims motion tracking at more than 1,000x fewer GPU-hours through a differentiable learned world model. A memory-augmented action-conditioned world model (oral, 2606.18960) keeps occluded objects across long rollouts ([alphaXiv orals](https://www.alphaxiv.org/conferences/corl/2026/oral)). Posters include WAM-STEP, Causal WAMs that learn from failed rollouts, MoRE, and a spotlight on poisoning world models to compromise robot learning pipelines ([portal](https://hongsong-wang.github.io/CoRL2026)). None of these reports a breakdown by object or by combination, so evidence that world models improve compositional generalization specifically is thin.

RL on top of pretrained policies is the second pillar. Alongside RL Token, the highlights are **Learning While Deploying** (2605.00416, AgiBot), which reports 95% average success over eight tasks from fleet-scale offline-to-online RL on a pi0.5 flow VLA, with its CoRL acceptance unconfirmed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00416)); **Flow Policy Gradients** (oral) for likelihood-free RL on flow policies; **Play2Perfect** (oral, about 33x sample efficiency on dexterous insertion); RAI Institute's **ExpertGen**, which runs RL on diffusion-policy initial noise ([RAI](https://rai-inst.com/resources/blog/corl-2026-publication-and-workshop-round-up/)); and **FlowDAgger** (spotlight), which turns human corrections into latent-space adaptation of frozen generative policies. Data scale is the third pillar. EgoScale, Being-H0.5, Ego2Robot (spotlight), HumanScale (2606.20521, +90% OOD real success from egocentric pretraining at matched data; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20521)) and MolmoBot's synthetic data all push it. The oral **"Diversity You Can Actually Measure"** proposes a dataset-diversity metric ([portal](https://hongsong-wang.github.io/CoRL2026)) and is worth reading against the reader's partner-count result.

Continual and few-shot adaptation results run both ways. "Pretrained VLAs are Surprisingly Resistant to Forgetting" (2603.03818, venue not stated) finds that pi0 and GR00T N1.5 forget far less than small BC policies and sometimes not at all under small replay buffers ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03818)). A real-world 10-task benchmark (2605.26820), by contrast, shows naive sequential fine-tuning forgetting catastrophically ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26820v2)). **Memory Anchors** (poster, 2608.26545) picks a small set of old-task experiences to anchor replay on LIBERO ([portal](https://hongsong-wang.github.io/CoRL2026)).

## Benchmarks saturate while robustness suites collapse

The **benchmark-audit oral** (Jiang, Tan, Wheeler et al., TTIC/UChicago, 2606.04233) is the most consequential evaluation paper for anyone reporting LIBERO numbers. A **0.09B DINO+MLP probe with no language encoder scores 99.0 / 100.0 / 98.8 / 92.4 on LIBERO Spatial / Object / Goal / Long**, against best reported scores of 99.8 / 100.0 / 99.5 / 98.8. **Only 19.8% of LIBERO state-of-the-art claims are statistically significant**, and redrawing initial states moves success by less than one point ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04233)). On CALVIN, resampling block poses within the training range drops X-VLA's ABC-to-D score from 4.17 to 3.14. SimplerEnv resists the probe (0.0%) but has only 19.7% significant claims. RoboCasa (53.3%) and RoboTwin 2.0 (73.7%) pass more of the audit's checks. The authors recommend per-instance reporting, paired tests, Wilson or Newcombe intervals, and test sets sized to the gain being claimed ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04233)). Whenever the reader's suite descends from LIBERO-Goal, these recommendations apply directly.

Robustness suites keep showing collapse. In **LIBERO-Plus** (CVPR 2026), pi0 falls from 94.2% to 15.8% under camera changes and to 6.6% under robot-initial-state changes. Language perturbations cause small drops, because the models ignore language ([arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626v3)). **LIBERO-PRO** reports models above 90% collapsing to 0.0% under perturbed objects, initial states and instructions, and outputs that do not change under nonsense instructions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03827)). On RoboCasa365, the aggregator leaderboard puts the **best Composite-Unseen score at 5.4%** (unverified; [benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/robocasa365/)). Sim benchmarks that need no real robot include LIBERO-Plus, RoboCasa365, RoboDojo ([code](https://github.com/RoboDojo-Benchmark/RoboDojo)), and the BEHAVIOR and RoboEval challenge tracks run by a CoRL workshop ([workshop](https://corl2026-realworld-robot-learning.github.io/)). For evaluation in learned world models, Mem-World reports r = 0.97 against real success ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18960)) and RoboWorld costs about 100 H100-hours for eight policies ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01060)).

Outside VLAs, the visible standouts are humanoids, touch and dexterity. RoboNaldo reports humanoid soccer shots at 13.10 m/s and SuperMimic, GRAIL and Flow Policy Gradients cover humanoid control. Tactile and force work includes FACTR 2, which estimates external torque without force sensors, and PACE, which separates steady contact from collisions. Dexterity papers include OPENDEXGRASP and Play2Perfect, and navigation includes Meta and NYU's hierarchical planning with latent world models ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/conferences/corl/2026); [alphaXiv orals](https://www.alphaxiv.org/conferences/corl/2026/oral)). Coverage of these areas reached titles and abstracts only.

## Composition fails at binding, and 2026 papers converge on the reader's diagnosis

### Held-out pairings collapse while demonstrated pairings stay near perfect

The 2026 benchmarks agree that pretrained VLAs execute demonstrated combinations almost perfectly and fail on held-out ones. **CRAFT** (Yang, Na, Cho, Yoon, KAIST, 2610.00524) builds LIBERO Pick-Place with 16 cube-plate combinations, demonstrates only the 4 same-color ones, and holds out the other 12. Success on held-out combinations after plain full fine-tuning is **8.4% for pi0.5, 2.8% for pi0 and 1.1% for GR00T N1.7**, against 94-100% on demonstrated ones. LoRA fine-tuning lifts pi0.5 to 46.3% but leaves pi0 at 1.0% and GR00T at 2.6% ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00524)). In CRAFT's Appendix A on LIBERO-Goal, the released checkpoints average **50.9% (pi0.5), 2.9% (pi0), 19.5% (pi0-FAST) and 2.0% (GR00T N1.7)** on five hand-chosen undemonstrated combinations. **MESA / BiMESA** (Wilcox ... Garg, CoRL 2026) includes a Composite suite of unseen combinations of in-distribution subtasks. pi0.5 drops from 62.8% in-distribution to 46.7% on composites, and GR00T-N1.6 from 25.0% to 15.0%. Yet pi0.5 still follows language on **81.5%** of composite episodes, which the authors read as an execution gap rather than a comprehension gap ([MESA](https://www.pair.toronto.edu/MESA/)).

"Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding" (Emukpere, Deffayet, Renders, Naver Labs, 2602.24143) gives the cleanest single-variable test. Every object and every location appears in training, and only the pairings are held out. **SmolVLA scores 44% on seen pairings and 0% on held-out ones; reach accuracy falls from 100% to 2%.** pi0.5 manages 10% under large jitter, and scaling from 10k to 100k demonstrations gives no gain ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.24143)). With a single object on the table success returns, so the failure lies in target selection rather than control. **LIBERO-CF** ("When Vision Overrides Language", CoRL spotlight, 2602.17659) poses counterfactual instructions in unchanged LIBERO scenes. pi0.5 completes only 13.2% of faithful counterfactual tasks, while 60.9% of its episodes complete the biased, habitual task ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17659)). Its Table 1 pairs pi0.5's 96.9% on standard LIBERO with 51.7% in the vision-only condition, so about half of LIBERO is solved without reading the instruction. RoboCombine (2610.15425) and ACG-Bench (2610.06184) find similar collapses for skill sequences and arm-wise compositions: 7.7% ordered success on recombinations, and 2.94% for single pi0.5 on unseen dual-arm compositions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15425); [OpenTrain](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/arm-wise-compositional-generalization-in-dual-arm-vision-language-action-models--arxiv-2610.06184/)).

### Vision shortcuts and scene-bound motor programs explain the failures

The papers name one cause in several forms: **a vision shortcut**. When the scene or object predicts the instruction, the policy stops reading the instruction. LangForce (ICML 2026, 2601.15197) formalizes this as "information collapse", where the conditional mutual information between instruction and action vanishes. Its fix, maximizing the action-instruction likelihood ratio, raises SimplerEnv-WidowX from 55.2 to 66.5 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15197v5)). Grant, Zhao and Wang (2603.19233) supply the most mechanistic evidence. Injecting source-episode activations at **pi0.5 action-expert layers 16-17 reproduces the source task's motion in 99.6% of 1,968 cross-task episodes**, bound to absolute workspace coordinates. The VLM pathway selects the goal and the expert generates the motor program. Every architecture tested encodes the instruction, but four of five weight it below vision when the scene identifies the goal. With a null prompt, pi0.5 scores near zero on LIBERO-Goal and 62% on LIBERO-Spatial ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19233); code at [action-atlas](https://github.com/CWRU-AISM/action-atlas)). Dr. VLA (2603.19183) finds that most SAE features in VLAs encode memorized demonstration sequences, and that diverse data or knowledge insulation yields more general features ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19183)). Singh et al. (2605.30695) report that ablating a 4-D "primitive subspace" halves few-shot transfer to held-out object-task combinations (0.66 to 0.34 for pi0.5). Their figures carry an "illustrative pending verification" caption, so treat the result with care ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30695)).

**CRAFT's Table A.4** gives the failure breakdown closest to the reader's observation. On held-out LIBERO-Goal combinations, pi0.5 performs the instructed task in 50.9% of rollouts and in **38.4% completes a demonstrated task that shares the instructed object**: it grasps the right object and takes it to that object's trained partner. GR00T N1.7 behaves the same way (39.3% share-object, 25.5% share-target), as does pi0-FAST (41.9% share-object). pi0 instead mostly finishes a task that shares the target (48.8%) ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00524)). The share-object pattern for pi0.5 and GR00T is the reader's "grasp right, place wrong" result described as whole-task outcomes. No paper read reports the reader's phase decomposition, with grasp correct on nearly every rollout and placement failing.

### Partner count predicts held-out success in small policies

No paper measures the reader's "object seen with one target versus two" effect on a pretrained VLA. Wang et al. ("Diagnosing Compositional Generalization in Sequential Robot Tasks", portal spotlight 36, arXiv 2607.29687) come closest. In a custom transformer flow policy with frozen DINOv2 on robomimic tasks, **OOD success rises with the number of seen instruction pairs per held-out task: 0.83%, 23.93% and 82.29% at B = 4, 8 and 16**, while in-distribution loss stays flat. A well-chosen orthogonal set of 16 tasks reaches 78.2% OOD against 78.6% for training on every task, using a quarter of the tasks ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29687)). The authors conclude that "sparse coverage causes instruction-steering failure, not missing skills". Qi et al. ("Scale Up Strategically", 2607.21582, NVIDIA/Northeastern, venue unstated) train on the diagonal of each factor-pair grid, which is CRAFT's one-partner design generalized. Adding one column that varies the dominant factor raises **real GR00T-N1.7 success from 22.9% to 54.2%** on one task family, and the method matches baselines with half the demonstrations. They also rank factor dominance as color ≥ object ≥ spatial ≥ verb ≥ size ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21582)). Under this lens, CRAFT's benchmark sits at the one-partner extreme of the reader's axis: every cube color has exactly one plate. The reader's 1-versus-2 comparison on frozen pi0.5 and GR00T would therefore be the first pretrained-VLA point on a curve that Wang and Qi have drawn only for small policies or for data allocation.

### How each close paper overlaps with the reader's findings and edit

The reader has three findings (grasp-right-place-wrong on unseen pairings; one-partner objects fail more than two-partner ones; hiding the held object's identity from the place skill repairs the failure) and a planned edit (grasp-event prompt switching, silhouettes for every non-active entity, and later keyed per-role, per-object adapters). Several points follow from the table. First, **AC-VLA is the most direct prior art for the reader's third finding**. Masking the wrist view while placing removes the held object from the place computation, and AC-VLA reports a 27-point Goal-OOD gain on pi0.5. The reader's version differs in three testable ways: it works on a frozen model, it hides identity at the level of the object, and it keeps geometry as a silhouette. AC-VLA has no ablation that separates identity from geometry, so whether its gain comes from hiding identity or from removing wrist-view geometry is open. Second, **ReGuide is the training-free baseline to beat**: 94.5% on a frozen pi0.5, with a GR00T N1.7 variant at 69.1%. It gets there with external motion transport. An input-only edit that approaches those numbers would be the stronger scientific claim, because it would locate the failure in what the place skill sees. Third, **ACT-VLA and AC-VLA share the reader's pi0.5 Goal-OOD baseline of 46.6**. The reader's numbers become directly comparable only if the held-out re-pairing list matches, and neither paper's text gives that list. Fourth, CRAFT's FT(Frozen) result and its CAG-VA baseline help pi0.5 but barely help pi0 or GR00T. That suggests training-free fixes depend on pi0.5's VLM language path. If the reader's identity hiding repairs GR00T as well as pi0.5, that is evidence the mechanism is the object channel rather than the language channel.

### Identity hiding, phase switching and adapter libraries leave the role-structured edit unoccupied

Hiding distractors is a crowded area, but none of the methods replaces non-active entities with identity-free silhouettes. **CGVD** (2603.10340) inpaints distractors once on the first frame and raises pi0 on SimplerEnv from 43.0% to 77.5% ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10340)). **OBEYED-VLA** (2512.22519) uses set-of-mark selection of task-relevant masks and raises pi0 from 5% to 85% with seen distractors ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22519)). **TAG** (2603.24584) is a warning for the reader's plan. **Per-frame gray or black masking, or inpainting, on pi0.5 collapses LIBERO average success to about 64% (LIBERO-Long 12.6-13.8%)**, while a static background holds 97.85%. The authors blame temporal inconsistency ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24584)). Silhouettes therefore need masks that stay stable from frame to frame. The phase switch also has a published caveat in Wang et al.: a policy that cannot see inactive instructions fails whenever an earlier step depends on a later goal. The reader's single pick-place pairings probably avoid this, but multi-step tasks would hit it. Phase switching at gripper events exists only at inference: VP-VLA, plus snippet-level ForeTime-VLA (2608.20735) and ActionGround (2609.33256) ([search](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20735)). No paper trains a grasp-event-switched conditioning end to end with role-specific visibility.

Adapter libraries do not compose. **CORAL** (2603.09298) keys one LoRA expert per task by instruction and swaps it in under 100 ms. It reaches 98.4% on LIBERO with pi0.5, but each adapter belongs to one whole task and no adapters are composed ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09298)). **MAST-VLA** (2610.32312) routes LoRA-MoE experts by seven coarse skill categories and reaches a CALVIN average length of 4.65 without testing unseen skill combinations ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32312)). The structured note reports MAST-VLA's arXiv page as saying "Accepted to CoRL 2026", but the full-text read found no venue. PhaseLoRA keys adapters by motion statistics. A library keyed by (role, object) and composed across unseen pairings, the reader's later step, goes beyond all three. Among the CoRL papers seen, the closest structured representations are ObstaDiff (poster 253: target, obstacle and background roles for diffusion policies), Phase-Conditioned Affordance Fields (poster 157), PACE's temporal-role tactile conditioning (oral), and Learning Additively Compositional Latent Actions (poster 208) ([portal](https://hongsong-wang.github.io/CoRL2026)). None reports held-out pairings.

### Frontier-model agent stacks move binding out of the motor policy

Code-as-policy and agent stacks sidestep binding: the planner names entities and the motor primitive never has to infer them. **Harness VLA** (2607.08448) wraps a frozen pi0.5 as a `vla_act` call inside an LLM-planned program of analytic primitives. On LIBERO-Pro it reaches **82.4% with Claude Code and 72.1% with Codex, against 50.0% for pi0.5 alone**, and a GPT-6 Astra planner reaches 92.63% in an appendix table. With high reasoning, though, the Codex planner logs 529 s of planner time in the latency table (78.5% success), against 344 s with reasoning off (62.5%) ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08448); code at [RLinf/RPent](https://github.com/RLinf/RPent)). Harness VLA still passes a task prompt to the VLA, so identity reaches the motor model. **CaP-X / CaP-Agent0** (2603.22435) beats pi0.5 on LIBERO-PRO instruction perturbations (0.17 vs 0.00 on Goal) but loses on position perturbations (0.26 vs 0.38) ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22435); MIT code at [cap-x](https://github.com/capgym/cap-x)). RHO (2606.16458) reports 45.0 on LIBERO-PRO against 12.83 for pi0.5 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16458)). Further agent stacks exist: URAI (2609.39018, RoboDojo 18.0% to 53.0%), "Agent as Policy" (2609.12541), EMERGE-Policy (2608.29896, LIBERO-Plus 93.9%, snippet only) and FAEA (2601.20334, Claude Agent SDK, 84.9% LIBERO with privileged state) ([URAI](https://www.roboticscenter.ai/research/papers/make-code-as-policy-great-again-frontier-agents-write-call-and-evolve-robot-tools-2609); [AaP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12541); [EMERGE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29896); [FAEA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20334)). The CoRL spotlight Playful Agentic Robot Learning builds a persistent code-skill library and reports +20.6 and +17.0 over CaP-Agent0 ([portal](https://hongsong-wang.github.io/CoRL2026)). "GPT-GEAR" could not be found. The nearest match is 2610.28259, "Large Multimodal Models for Direct Robot Control: GPT-6 as a Beginning" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28259)). Taken together, these stacks confirm the reader's premise at system scale, since performance rises when something outside the motor policy does the binding. They do not show whether binding can be fixed inside a single VLA.

## What remains open, and what to read and run first

Five questions remain open after this scan. First, **the partner-count law on pretrained VLAs**: Wang et al. and Qi et al. show coverage effects in small policies and in data allocation, CRAFT fixes one partner per object, and no one reports 1-versus-2-partner held-out success on frozen pi0.5 or GR00T. Second, **grasp-versus-place decomposition**: CRAFT's share-object column and MESA's language-following rate approach it, but no paper splits held-out failure by phase on frozen models. Third, **identity versus geometry**: AC-VLA, LiLo-VLA, CGVD, OBEYED-VLA and S2 all remove whole views or tokens, so no ablation shows whether hiding the label alone, while keeping shape and position, explains the gain. Fourth, **transfer across architectures**: CRAFT's evidence implies training-free fixes are pi0.5-specific, and GR00T 2's single-transformer design removes the VLM/expert seam that interpretability work (Grant et al.) relies on. Fifth, **composition across keyed adapters**: neither CORAL, MAST-VLA nor PhaseLoRA tests unseen pairings. The open risks to the planned edit are documented too. TAG shows the damage done by temporally unstable masks, and Wang et al. show the bottleneck that hiding inactive instructions creates when later goals constrain earlier steps.

Read **CRAFT**, **AC-VLA** and **ReGuide** first. Together they define the setting, the closest repair and the baseline to beat. Read **Wang et al.** and **Qi et al.** next for the partner-count framing, then **Grant et al.** and **LIBERO-CF** for mechanism, **TAG** for the masking risk, and **the benchmark audit** for statistical reporting. Among things to run, **MESA's environment and checkpoints are released** ([code](https://github.com/pairlab/mesa-env); [weights](https://huggingface.co/collections/albertwilcox/mesa)) and supply a composite split with GR00T-N1.6 and pi0.5 baselines. CAG's guidance rule (a = a_uncond + w(a_cond − a_uncond), w = 1.5) is a few lines to reimplement as an object-channel analogue. LangForce ([code](https://github.com/ZGC-EmbodyAI/LangForce)) and Grant et al.'s action-atlas give a training-objective baseline and a probing toolkit. CaP-X and Harness VLA's RPent provide agent baselines on LIBERO-PRO. Among models, GR00T N1.7 (open code and weights) and MolmoAct2 (open weights, code and data) are the runnable VLAs. GR00T 2 code is promised for December, and pi0.7 is available only through the API. CRAFT, AC-VLA and Singh et al. promise code; ReGuide, ACT-VLA, LIBERO-CF, S2, VP-VLA's tables and PhaseLoRA's repository were not confirmed as available. The repository contents of every linked repo above were not opened.

## Conclusion

CoRL 2026 gives the reader both independent confirmation and a priority race. The diagnosis the reader reached by measurement, that a VLA grasps correctly and then lets the held object's identity choose the destination, now has behavioral (CRAFT, MESA), single-variable (Brittle Grounding), counterfactual (LIBERO-CF) and mechanistic (Grant et al.) support from separate groups. The repairs, however, are converging from three directions: data (ACT-VLA, Qi et al.), training-time masking (AC-VLA) and external motion correction (ReGuide). Within months, a reviewer will ask why an input-level edit is needed when ReGuide already reaches 94.5% on frozen pi0.5. The answer the reader can give is the one none of these papers can: a controlled separation of identity from geometry, a partner-count curve on pretrained VLAs, and a phase-resolved failure account. These turn an engineering fix into a theory of where binding lives.

GR00T 2 changes how general that theory has to be. With the frozen-VLM seam gone and binding handed to external agents and image marks, a claim that VLAs need role-structured inputs should be tested on a world-action model as soon as GR00T 2's code ships in December. A theory that holds across the dual-system line, pi0.5 and a joint video-action transformer would cover all three architectures the field is betting on. A theory that fails on the world-action model would show that GR00T 2's design binds referents in a different way.
