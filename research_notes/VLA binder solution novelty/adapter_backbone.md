# Novelty check: binding adapter into deep image K/V of a frozen VLA (Part 1) and base-VLM-as-binder crutch (Part 2)

Scope: through Oct 2026. Sources fetched this session are marked [fetched]. Items marked [prior knowledge] come from the researcher's background knowledge of well-known papers and were NOT re-fetched this session. Check their IDs before citing.

## Part 1: Who conditions frozen VLAs on masks, points, boxes, traces or visual prompts, and is a binding-only adapter into deep image K/V, trained from counterfactual same-scene/different-target pairs, prior art?

### Takeaway
Conditioning VLAs on role masks or visual prompts is crowded prior art, but nearly all of it works at the input (pixel overlays, extra mask channels, drawn traces/points) or retrains the policy on prompt-conditioned data. The closest frozen-policy internal edits are (a) a whole-prefix KV swap used as a causal diagnostic and intervention on pi0.5 (2609.39971), (b) a mask-weighted residual added to post-projector visual tokens of frozen OpenVLA (2610.19651, recognition only, no robot rollouts), and (c) zero-init residual injection into a frozen DiT (2603.12772). I found no paper that trains a small mask-to-K/V adapter for the deep image tokens of a frozen flow VLA, supervised by counterfactual same-scene/different-target pairs, so the bindings it writes make the frozen policy act on the supplied target. Verdict: **partial novelty**. The ingredients (mask conditioning, frozen-policy adapters, KV/prefix editing, counterfactual pairs) each have prior art. Their combination, with the edit placed only on deep image K/V and trained once per policy with no target-task demos, appears new.

### Cited Findings

**Closest internal-edit works (frozen policy)**
- 2609.39971 (Chen et al., 30 Sep 2026, "When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories") [fetched]. It ranks candidate prefixes by "cosine distance in the final-layer prefix key–value (KV) representation read by π0.5's action tokens", and the executed task ranks first in 34/45 cases. Its closed-loop intervention replaces pi0.5's prefix-KV cache with one computed from the target task's observation and instruction. The arm then ends nearer the target object in 109/200 rollouts vs 0/200 for controls, and source-task completion drops from 200/200 to 0. For GR00T-N1.7 it swaps the backbone feature sequence, and 121/200 rollouts end nearer the target. Its method, ECT, is counterfactual paired training: the instruction stays fixed and the scene changes by mirror or shift, (s,ℓ,a) and (s′,ℓ,a′), and both imitation losses are summed. That is the reverse of same-scene/different-target, and it **fine-tunes the policy**. The main text does not say which tokens or layers the swap patches; that is in Appendix E, which was not read. — [arXiv 2609.39971 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39971v1); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39971)
- SPECTRA-VLA 2610.19651 (Hsu, 14 Oct 2026) [fetched]. It adds a learned residual to the target's 256 post-projector visual tokens. The target mask is area-pooled to 16x16 occupancy weights, so only target tokens change, and the residual's norm is capped relative to the base token. The visual encoder and action decoder of OpenVLA-7B and SpatialVLA-4B stay frozen. It is **not K/V and not deep layers**, it carries spectral identity rather than a role binding, it trains on category labels with no actions, and it reports **no closed-loop robot success**. — [arXiv 2610.19651](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19651v1)
- PVI 2603.12772 [fetched, search snippet only]. It injects layer-wise hidden states into a frozen DiT through zero-initialized residual mappings, so training starts from the original policy's behavior. The snippet shows no object-mask conditioning. — [arXiv 2603.12772](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12772v1)
- Fast-VLA 2610.21389 [snippet]. A frozen 4B VLM runs once per episode to cache the instruction and initial views as fixed multi-layer key–value task memory. This is KV caching for speed, not binding. — [arXiv 2610.21389](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21389)
- "When Do Frozen VLMs Respond to Image-Free Object-Token Edits?" 2609.03429 (3 Sep 2026) [fetched]. It edits object-level tokens fed to frozen VLMs on remote-sensing VQA, not VLAs. It finds that edits take effect only after "explicit edit teaching", which is supporting evidence that a frozen model needs a trained interface before token edits change behavior. — [arXiv 2609.03429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03429)

**Frozen VLA + mask/visual prompt at the input (training-free)**
- Bring My Cup / VAP 2512.20014 (Lee, Mo, Han; v3 June 2026) [fetched]. It is a "training-free perceptual adapter" on frozen pi0 and pi0.5. It overlays a semi-transparent tint on the target's segmentation mask (from Grounding DINO + DINOv2 matching + SAM2) and rewrites "my X" as "the [color] X". Results: WidowX shaver pick 0% to 71.3%; real-world selection with pi0.5 30% to 80%. A **Soft Prompt baseline**, a learned token embedding per object on a frozen VLA, gives only marginal gains. That is a published negative datapoint for learned-token conditioning. — [arXiv 2512.20014](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20014)
- ReGuide 2609.38616 (Zhang et al., 29 Sep 2026) [fetched abstract]. It is a "training-free wrapper" around a frozen VLA. Using object poses from a grounding module, it guides the end-effector toward demonstration-supported configurations, then hands control back to the frozen policy. It overrides actions and edits no representations. It reports up to +56.8 pp in simulation and +75.0 pp on a real robot under compositional shifts. — [arXiv 2609.38616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38616)
- VLA Grounder 2607.04517 [snippet]. It optimizes the language-conditioning space of black-box VLAs. — [arXiv 2607.04517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04517)

**Mask/point/trace-conditioned VLAs trained with the prompt (not frozen)**
- RoboGround [prior knowledge: arXiv 2504.21530]. It conditions the policy on target-object and placement masks plus language in RoboCasa. A later paper drops language when visual prompts are supplied (2610.01794). — [arXiv 2610.01794](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01794v1)
- DeicticVLA 2608.28108 [snippet]. It separates two kinds of visual prompting: overlays on RGB, and separate-channel mask prompting fused at the feature level. It cites IA-VLA graying out the background. — [arXiv 2608.28108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28108)
- "Point What You Mean" 2512.18933 [snippet]. It tests object-only masking, where the background is blacked out. — [arXiv 2512.18933](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18933v2)
- Embodied-R1 2508.13998 [snippet]. It is a 3B Qwen2.5-VL pointing model whose referring-expression grounding outputs a point inside the target mask. It is a separate reasoner, not an adapter. — [arXiv 2508.13998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13998)
- Pointing-VLA 2608.23138 [snippet]. It builds on Embodied-R1 and adds heads for points, affordance heatmaps and visual traces. — [arXiv 2608.23138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23138)
- TraceVLA (2412.10345), RT-Trajectory (2311.01977), HAMSTER (2502.05485), CoT-VLA (2503.22020), MOKA (2403.03174), GraspVLA (2505.03233), PIVOT (2402.07872) [prior knowledge, not fetched]. They draw traces, sketches or marks on the input image, have a VLM predict a path, subgoal image or keypoints, or (GraspVLA) predict the grasp box inside the model. All of them either fine-tune the policy on the prompt format or use the VLM directly as a planner. None writes into a frozen policy's internals. PIVOT appeared in search results: [arXiv 2402.07872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07872)

**General frozen-backbone adapters**
- VLA-Adapter 2509.09372 [snippet] and VLA-GSE 2605.06175 [snippet] are adapter/PEFT methods that teach actions. They do not inject bindings. — [VLA-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09372); [VLA-GSE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06175)
- Prefix tuning (Li & Liang 2101.00190) and LoRA (2106.09685) [prior knowledge] are the generic template a reviewer will cite: learned per-layer K/V prefixes or low-rank updates on a frozen model.

### Inferences
- The fact that pi0.5 binds through prefix KV, so that swapping the cache redirects the arm, is established by 2609.39971. A reviewer can argue that the proposed adapter learns a mask-conditioned, partial version of that swap. The proposal must position itself as **the operator that 2609.39971's diagnostic implies is possible**: a role-specific edit that needs no target-task observation, and it must be shown to preserve the motor program, the "HOW". 2609.39971's injection drove source-task completion from 200/200 to 0, and it moved the arm closer to the target without reporting target-task success.
- Two baselines are mandatory: VAP-style tint overlays (training-free, frozen pi0.5, strong numbers) and RoboGround-style mask channels. If a pixel overlay already gives most of the gain, the K/V adapter must win on something the overlay cannot do. Candidates are part or sub-region roles, goal regions, no visible artifacts in the image, and multi-role scenes. VAP's own Soft-Prompt result (learned token, marginal gains) helps the proposal: it shows that the input-token route underperforms. The proposal would then show that the deep K/V route works where a soft token does not.
- Same-scene/different-target counterfactual pairs as the adapter's training signal: ECT uses the opposite pairing (same instruction, different scene) and fine-tunes the policy. I found no paper that uses same-scene/different-target pairs to train a frozen-policy adapter.
- **Strongest reviewer objection:** "This is prefix-tuning/KV editing conditioned on a mask. 2609.39971 already showed that swapping pi0.5's prefix KV redirects the arm, and VAP already gets the frozen pi0.5 to the masked object with a tint, no training. The binding comes from the external segmenter, so the contribution is engineering." Rebuttal points needed: (i) beat VAP and mask-channel baselines on a matched benchmark; (ii) show that the edit is confined to layers 10-17 image K/V and changes WHERE while preserving HOW, for example the motor program measured on the source task and success on unseen bindings; (iii) show that one adapter transfers across tasks with no target demos; (iv) ablate the layer range against input-token and final-layer injection.

### Gaps
- I did not read Appendix E of 2609.39971, so whether its KV swap patches image tokens, language tokens, or specific layers is unknown. This matters for how close it is.
- The RoboGround, GraspVLA, TraceVLA, HAMSTER, CoT-VLA, MOKA and RT-Trajectory details and IDs come from prior knowledge and were not verified this session.
- I found no paper on object-centric slot/token injection into a frozen flow VLA specifically. Absence in about 15 searches is weak evidence, not proof.
- IA-VLA appeared only as a citation inside 2608.28108. Its ID was not found.

## Part 2: Do VLAs lose the base VLM's referring-expression grounding after robot fine-tuning, and has anyone used the base VLM's own grounding as an external binder? What are PaliGemma's RefCOCO numbers?

### Takeaway
Many papers show that VLAs lose VLM abilities after robot fine-tuning, but the measurements are VQA/MMMU/POPE, attribute-selection or linear probes. **None that I found measures referring-expression grounding (RefCOCO-style detect/segment prompts) before vs after robot fine-tuning, and none uses the VLA's own pre-fine-tuning base VLM as the grounding module for the fine-tuned policy.** The nearest relatives are architectural: TwinBrainVLA keeps a frozen generalist copy of the VLM fused inside the policy, and alignment/co-training methods regularize toward the base. Verdict: **the measurement (grounding lost, pi0/pi0.5 vs PaliGemma on RefCOCO-style prompts) looks novel; the remedy (base VLM as a frozen external binder) is partial**. Frozen-generalist and external-detector pipelines are common, so a reviewer will call it engineering unless the measurement itself is the contribution.

### Cited Findings

**Forgetting evidence (no RefCOCO measured)**
- Knowledge Insulation (pi0.5 + KI) 2505.23705 [fetched]. The authors write that "gradients from randomly initialized robotics specific adapters unfavorably interact with the pre-trained VLM weights" and that with KI "the VLM backbone is thus not damaged by backpropagation from the action expert". Co-training data includes captioning, VQA and **object localization with bounding boxes** (indoor/household web data, App. A.3). The paper reports **no VLM benchmark before/after** and measures only robot success and language-following rates. — [arXiv 2505.23705](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23705)
- Don't Blind Your VLA 2510.25616 (Kachaev et al., Oct 2025) [fetched]. It compares OpenVLA-7B with its base Prismatic-7B on the VL-Think board-selection tasks: Arrow 0.26 vs 0.47, Public-info 0.24 vs 0.62, Weather 0.32 vs 0.62, Shape 0.40 vs 0.59, Color 0.69 vs 0.69. Linear probe on ImageNet-100: SFT 77.48% vs pretrained 79.88%. Its fix aligns layer-16 hidden states to frozen C-RADIOv3 (cosine, λ=0.2). It has **no referring-expression or localization metric**, only qualitative attention maps, and does not test pi0. — [arXiv 2510.25616](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25616)
- TwinBrainVLA 2601.14133 (Jan 2026) [fetched abs]. It runs a frozen generalist "Left Brain" VLM alongside a trainable "Right Brain" fused by an asymmetric MoT. It is the **closest "base VLM as crutch" architecture**, but the frozen copy is fused inside the policy and trained jointly with it, not used as an external binder. Per a search snippet, the paper reports that standard VLA training collapses POPE from about 89% to near zero. The abstract page does not show this number. — [arXiv 2601.14133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14133); snippet via [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.14133)
- RoboSemanticBench 2606.02277 (June 2026) [fetched]. It evaluates 9 VLAs including pi0 and pi0.5 on question-to-target selection. Most score near or below chance at selecting the right target. pi0.5 is best, with TSR 21.8% and nSG 5.2. It does **not test the base VLM**, and VQA co-training lowered TSR from 10.7% to 8.2%. — [arXiv 2606.02277](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02277v1)
- StarVLA 2604.05014 [search snippet]. It states that action-only fine-tuning loses visual/language abilities within thousands of steps, with weaker object grounding, and that co-training with grounding data counters this. I did not verify the numbers. — [arXiv 2604.05014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05014)
- InstructVLA 2507.17520 [snippet]. OpenVLA fine-tuned on VLA-IT gains little on situated reasoning, which the authors attribute to forgetting. — [arXiv 2507.17520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17520)
- VLM2VLA / Actions as Language 2509.22195 (ICLR 2026) [snippet]. It writes actions as language plus LoRA and keeps more than 85% of base VQA performance. — [arXiv 2509.22195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22195)
- ChatVLA 2502.14420 [snippet]. Phased Alignment Training + MoE gives MMMU 6x over prior VLAs and MMStar 47.2% (an AlphaXiv summary says 37.4%, a conflict; prefer the abstract). No RefCOCO numbers were found. — [arXiv 2502.14420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14420v2)
- Counterpoint: "Fine-tuning VLMs Without Forgetting Is Easier Than You Think" (OpenReview) argues that forgetting is less severe than assumed and can be fixed with recipe changes. — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf/278c682d3fdae858e47cc6e1111e5d282dd787fa.pdf)
- Anecdotal: openpi issue #768 reports that a pi0.5 fine-tuned on about 90 minutes of data ignores prompt variations. — [GitHub openpi #768](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/768)

**PaliGemma grounding numbers**

- PaliGemma 2 (2412.03555) RefCOCO by size/resolution, per a search snippet of the model card: testA 224: 3B 75.7, 10B 77.2, 28B 76.8; 448: 3B 78.6, 10B 79.7 (testB 76.2, val 78.2), 28B 79.3/74.8/77.3. - The PaliGemma 2 mix blog shows detection/segmentation examples at 448 qualitatively, with no table. — [HF blog paligemma2mix](https://huggingface.co/blog/paligemma2mix)
- Backbone lineage [prior knowledge]: pi0 (2410.24164) initializes from PaliGemma-3B (pt-224). pi0.5 (2504.16054) also starts from PaliGemma and co-trains on web data including object localization (confirmed for the KI recipe above). So the openpi base for pi0/pi0.5 is a **pt** checkpoint, not mix.

### Inferences
- The main empirical claim is "the base PaliGemma still grounds referring expressions; pi0.5's fine-tuned prefix does not". No paper measures it, so it is a defensible new measurement. The obvious confound: pi0.5 co-trains on bounding-box localization (KI recipe), so PI may argue that grounding is preserved there. The measurement must use the released openpi checkpoints (pi0.5_libero, etc.) and a matched prompt protocol (`detect X` / `segment X`) on the fine-tuned backbone.
- Which checkpoint is "base" matters. pi0's base is PaliGemma pt-224, whose zero-shot referring ability without transfer fine-tuning is weaker than the published fine-tuned RefCOCO mIoU of about 73 at 224. The only RefCOCO numbers published are single-task transfer numbers. If the proposal uses the PaliGemma (2) mix checkpoint as the binder, it is no longer "the policy's own base", and a reviewer will call it an external detector.
- Part/sub-region references: RefCOCO/+/g are whole-object references, and I found no PaliGemma evaluation on part references (handle, drawer front, left half of a region). Assume that is unsupported until it is measured.
- **Strongest reviewer objection:** "Using a VLM detector or segmenter to produce masks is standard engineering (Grounding DINO + SAM in VAP, grounding module in ReGuide, Embodied-R1 pointing). Calling it 'the backbone' adds nothing unless you show the fine-tuned backbone lost exactly this ability and that the base's grounding is better than an off-the-shelf detector." A second objection: TwinBrainVLA already keeps a frozen generalist copy of the VLM next to the policy.

### Gaps
- No RefCOCO or referring-expression before/after numbers for any VLA (pi0, pi0.5, OpenVLA, GR00T) were found. Either this is unmeasured or it sits in appendices I did not read (StarVLA, TwinBrainVLA, ChatVLA full tables).
- PaliGemma 2 mix RefCOCO numbers were not found on any primary page fetched. The PaliGemma 2 numbers above came from a search snippet.
- I found no data on PaliGemma part-level referring segmentation.
- GR00T-specific forgetting studies, beyond RoboSemanticBench including GR00T N1.7 and QwenGR00T, were not found.
