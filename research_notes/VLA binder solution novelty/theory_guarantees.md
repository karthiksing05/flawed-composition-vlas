# Theory and guarantees for compositional binding / grounding: novelty check (through Oct 2026)

Claims checked:
- (C1) Task success factorises as P(correct binding) x motor competence for language-conditioned robot policies.
- (C2) Systematicity guarantee: under "atom invariance" (each primitive's error given its atom is independent of the other atoms in the compound), a union bound gives binding error <= sum of atom errors for any unseen compound of seen atoms, independent of compound divergence. Holistic or exemplar (nearest-neighbour) binders' error grows with compound divergence.
- (C3) Split-conformal calibrated binding/grounding sets with abstention or clarification for robot instruction grounding.

Search budget: about 14 search/fetch calls. Sources marked "(background, not fetched)" come from prior knowledge, not from pages read this session. Verify them before citing.

## Q1. Closest theoretical results on compositional-generalisation guarantees. Does any give a union-bound guarantee for factorised referring-expression grounding?

### Takeaway
None of the theory papers checked gives a quantitative per-component (union-bound) error guarantee for unseen compounds. The strongest result, Wiedemer et al. 2307.05596, is an exact identification theorem: perfect fit on the training support implies perfect fit on unseen combinations. The authors state that a bounded-error version is future work. C2's union bound therefore reads as a quantitative, error-level relaxation of Wiedemer's "compositional support" logic. That is a small but real step. Its content sits almost entirely in the atom-invariance assumption.

### Cited Findings
- Wiedemer et al., "Compositional generalization from first principles" (2307.05596), Theorem 1. Ground truth f and model f̂ share a composition function C over a factorised latent Z = Z1 x ... x ZK. Assumptions: A2 compositional support (equal marginal support per slot), A3 sufficient support (a full-rank Jacobian condition), A4 an initial matching point, plus regularity. Guarantee: f = f̂ on P implies f = f̂ on Q. The result is **exact equality, not an error bound**, with no epsilon or per-component error terms. The authors say it holds "only if the learned model perfectly matches the ground-truth model" and list bounded generalisation error as future work. — [arXiv HTML 2307.05596](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05596)
- Ram, Klinger & Gray, "What makes Models Compositional? A Theoretical View" (2405.02350; preliminary version at the KBCG@IJCAI23 workshop). This is a Rademacher-style risk gap bound proportional to a "locus of influence" δ, a Lipschitz constant and C_N. It is **not a union bound over component errors**. The paper concedes that at high compositional complexity the bound "does not provide a favorable systematic generalization guarantee". It gives no modular-vs-holistic comparison of its own and only cites Jarvis et al. 2023 for that. — [arXiv 2405.02350](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02350)
- Yuanpeng Li, "A Theoretical Analysis of Provable Compositional Generalization..." (2505.02627; v2 Sep 2026; single author; arXiv only; Lean-4 verified). It gives a necessary and sufficient condition: structural alignment of the computational graph with the task hierarchy, plus "unambiguous minimized representations". From the abstract alone, I could not confirm any per-component error-sum bound. — [arXiv 2505.02627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02627)
- Uselis, Dittadi & Oh, "Compositional Generalization Requires Linear, Orthogonal Representations in Vision Embedding Models" (2602.24264). Three desiderata (divisibility, transferability, stability) are shown to **necessitate** linear per-concept decomposition with orthogonal components, along with dimension bounds. It is a necessity result, with no sufficiency guarantee, no quantitative error bound and no union bound. — [arXiv 2602.24264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24264)
- Lippl & Stachenfeld, "When does compositional structure yield compositional generalization? A kernel theory" (2405.16391, ICLR 2025). Kernel models on fixed compositional representations can only compute "conjunction-wise additive" functions: they assign a value to each combination seen in training and sum the values. The paper identifies the failure modes "memorization leak" and "shortcut bias", both driven by training-data statistics. This is the closest formal treatment of exemplar/kernel learners on novel combinations, but it is not a divergence-indexed error-growth theorem. — [arXiv 2405.16391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16391)
- Okawa, Lubana, Dick & Tanaka, "Compositional Abilities Emerge Multiplicatively" (2310.09336, NeurIPS 2023). Compositional performance emerges abruptly because of "multiplicative reliance on the performance of constituent tasks". This is the empirical and conceptual precedent for product-of-components success (relevant to C1), and the dual of a union bound on failure. — [arXiv 2310.09336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09336)
- Keysers et al., MCD (1912.09713). Compound divergence is a Chernoff-coefficient divergence over compound distributions, with atom divergence held low. Across architectures, compound divergence correlates strongly negatively with accuracy. This is empirical, with no theory explaining why. — [arXiv 1912.09713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.09713); [Google blog](https://research.google/blog/measuring-compositional-generalization/)
- A 2026 paper reports the opposite trend for Transformers ("the more compound divergence the better" in its setting). This conflicts with the MCD finding, and a reviewer may raise it against the "holistic error grows with divergence" half of C2. — [arXiv 2609.13144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13144) (snippet only; not fully read)
- Jarvis et al. 2023 (cited inside Ram et al.): modular functions generalise systematically on data with compositional sub-structure, and general non-modular functions do not. — cited via [arXiv 2405.02350](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02350)
- Abbe et al., "Generalization on the Unseen" (JMLR 25): OOD generalisation is only possible when the target has special structure (compositional, invariances). They study min-degree implicit bias on unseen Boolean inputs. — [JMLR 24-0220](http://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume25/24-0220/24-0220.pdf)
- "Modularity Trumps Invariance for Compositional Robustness" (2306.09005): an empirical modular-vs-monolithic comparison on compositional corruptions. — [arXiv 2306.09005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09005) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- **C2 verdict: partial; the bound itself is not novel.** Bounding P(any atom wrong) by the sum of the per-atom error rates is a textbook union bound. Wiedemer's compositional-support logic already says a slot-wise model generalises to unseen slot combinations. What C2 adds is (a) an error-level, non-asymptotic statement instead of exact identification, (b) its application to referring-expression and role binding in VLAs, and (c) the contrast with exemplar binders. I found no paper that states (a)+(b) together. I also found no theorem that proves (c), exemplar error increasing in compound divergence. Lippl & Stachenfeld's memorization leak is the closest formal analogue.
- The "independent of compound divergence" part follows directly from atom invariance. If each atom's conditional error does not depend on its context, then by assumption it cannot depend on how novel the compound is. The theorem is valid but carries no further content.
- **Strongest reviewer objection (C2):** "The union bound is trivial; atom invariance is the whole content, and it is untestable on unseen compounds. The assumption already states that context does not matter. It is also false for relational binding: in 'put A on B', swapping roles is an error no atom-wise bound sees unless atoms are role-tagged. Role tagging then moves the problem into the role assigner." A second objection: the bound is vacuous once K x ε approaches 1 (for example, 4 roles at 15% each gives 0.6).
- Mitigations: (i) state atom invariance as a measurable conditional-independence test on held-out compounds, and report the empirical gap from the bound. (ii) Prove or at least construct a lower bound showing nearest-neighbour binders' error rising with compound divergence, using kernel results in the style of Lippl & Stachenfeld. (iii) Treat role assignment as its own "atom" with its own error term.

### Gaps
- I did not read Dong & Ma, "First steps toward understanding the extrapolation of nonlinear models to unseen domains" (background: arXiv 2211.11719, ICLR 2023; not fetched), which gives additive-structure extrapolation guarantees. It may contain an additive bound worth citing.
- Lachapelle et al., "Additive decoders for latent variables identification and Cartesian-product extrapolation" (background: arXiv 2307.02598, NeurIPS 2023; not fetched) is another exact identification/extrapolation result, likely in the same category as Wiedemer.
- I could not verify Wiedemer et al.'s object-centric follow-up (background: arXiv 2310.05327, ICLR 2024; additive decoder + compositional consistency).
- Hupkes et al. 1908.08351 ("Compositionality decomposed") is a test taxonomy (systematicity/productivity/substitutivity/localism/overgeneralisation) with no guarantees. Not fetched this session.
- Ren et al. and Jarvis et al.'s primary papers were not fetched, so they appear here only as cited by Ram et al.

## Q2. Classic binding theory and LLM binding mechanisms: any formal holistic-vs-factorised results?

### Takeaway
Classic binding theory gives representational schemes and arguments for systematicity, not error guarantees. The LLM binding work is mechanistic, from causal-mediation experiments, with no theorems. None of the work checked bounds a factorised binder's error against a holistic one's.

### Cited Findings
- Feng & Steinhardt, "How do Language Models Bind Entities in Context?" (2310.17191, ICLR 2024). Binding-ID vectors are added to entity and attribute activations. The two properties tested are factorizability and position independence, both by causal mediation in Pythia and LLaMA. The IDs form a continuous subspace. This is an empirical mechanism, not a guarantee. — [arXiv 2310.17191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191)
- "Representational Analysis of Binding in Language Models" (2409.05448): a low-rank "Ordering ID" subspace as the prototype of binding IDs, with causal tests. Mechanistic, with no theorems. — [arXiv 2409.05448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.05448)
- Smolensky (1990) tensor product representations, Hummel & Holyoak (1997) LISA, and Plate's HRR/VSA (background, not fetched). These are role-filler binding schemes with exact or noisy unbinding. VSA capacity analyses give crosstalk-noise bounds per bound pair, which are the closest to "per-binding error" but concern representation capacity, not learned-binder generalisation. Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988) give the systematicity argument against holistic connectionism. It is philosophical, not quantitative.

### Inferences
- Factorised (role-filler) vs holistic binding is a long-standing cognitive-science debate. C2's contribution would be turning the systematicity argument into a quantitative statement that can be falsified. Frame it that way and cite Fodor & Pylyshyn and Smolensky as the conceptual precedent.
- A reviewer may say: "Feng & Steinhardt show LLM binding is already factorised (binding IDs), so VLAs inheriting a factorised binder is expected." This supports the paper's framing more than it threatens novelty.

### Gaps
- My search found no 2025-2026 follow-ups to binding IDs (the search tool may index recent preprints poorly). I also found no formal VSA-vs-learned-binder generalisation theorem.

## Q3. Conformal prediction for robot language grounding: is conformal calibration of a referring-expression binder (object + region role sets) with abstention prior art?

### Takeaway
Conformal sets with ask-for-help over candidate grounded actions are clear prior art: KnowNo (CoRL 2023), with IntroPlan (NeurIPS 2024) as a follow-up. KnowNo's options are effectively bindings ("pick X, place in Y"). What I did not find is conformal prediction applied to a VLA's internal binder over factorised role sets, that is, separate object and region sets composed into a joint set. Applying split-conformal sets to a new score function is routine, so the novelty margin is thin.

### Cited Findings
- KnowNo, Ren et al., "Robots That Ask For Help" (2307.01928, CoRL 2023). An LLM planner scores multiple-choice next-step options, and conformal prediction keeps those above a calibrated quantile. If more than one option remains, the robot asks for help. The paper claims 1-ε task-level coverage for both single-step and multi-step planning while minimising set size, which it equates with minimal help. Ambiguity types include spatial, numeric, preference and Winograd. — [PMLR v229 ren23a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)
- IntroPlan, "Aligning Robots' Uncertainty with Inherent Task Ambiguity" (NeurIPS 2024). It builds conformal sets over candidate user intents with P(z_test ∈ Ĝ) >= 1-ε. Introspective reasoning tightens the sets and reduces unnecessary clarification. — [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8451a20c5a7e0ee5671dda28f7daf7f3-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- ConformalNL2LTL (2504.21022): conformal correctness guarantees for translating natural-language instructions into temporal logic. This is grounding at the specification level. — [arXiv 2504.21022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21022)
- ENCP (2609.17499): episode-normalised conformal prediction for vision-and-language navigation. Only the reference list was visible, so the method is unverified. — [arXiv 2609.17499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17499)
- "Conformal Coverage Guarantees for Any Video Temporal Grounder" (2608.07434). A post-hoc, model-agnostic wrapper returns a temporal region containing the true moment with probability >= 1-α. It notes that RCPS and Learn-then-Test extend coverage to FNR and IoU losses. This is the closest grounding-specific conformal work, but it covers video time spans, not objects or roles. — [arXiv 2608.07434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07434)
- "The Yes-Man Syndrome: Benchmarking Abstention in Embodied Robotic Agents" (2605.20544). It defines a Reference Grounding abstention category (missing, ambiguous or underspecified referent, subjective intent). It is a benchmark and taxonomy, not a conformal method. — [arXiv 2605.20544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20544)
- Conformal work on VLAs: arXiv 2604.16677 applies conformal quantile regression to the deviation between sampled VLA actions and expert-like behaviour, plus a Mahalanobis execution monitor. The abstract states no formal coverage level, has no grounding/execution split and does not abstain or clarify. **Source conflict:** arXiv lists the title as "SentryVLA" (Lyu, Chen, Beksi), while a search aggregator labelled the same ID "ReconVLA". — [arXiv 2604.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16677); [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.16677)
- Learnable conformal abstention for LLMs/VLMs (2502.06884) and geometry-calibrated conformal abstention (2604.27914) are not robot-specific. 2606.16667 reports that conformal hallucination filters can need >80% abstention to keep hallucination below 5%, a cautionary number for abstention rates. — [arXiv 2502.06884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06884v1); [arXiv 2604.27914](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27914); [papers.cool 2606.16667](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2606.16667)
- Conformal object detection (box-level sets with a Bonferroni correction over coordinates) is established. — [PMLR v204 andeol23a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v204/andeol23a/andeol23a.pdf); [arXiv 2605.07549](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07549v1)

### Inferences
- **C3 verdict: partial, leaning prior art.** Conformal binding sets with ask-for-help appear in KnowNo and IntroPlan at the planner level. The new elements would be (a) calibration inside a VLA's binder, not an LLM planner; (b) role-factorised sets (object set x region set, possibly with a Bonferroni or union split of α across roles, which ties neatly to C2's union bound); and (c) distinguishing "binding set ambiguous -> clarify" from "binding confident but motor fails". I found no paper doing (b) for referring expressions.
- **Strongest reviewer objection (C3):** "This is KnowNo with a different score head. Split conformal on any classifier is standard." Mitigation: make the contribution the role-wise α allocation and its link to the C2 bound (joint coverage >= 1 - Σα_r). Show that marginal coverage holds on unseen compounds only when atom invariance holds, which turns the conformal sets into a test of the assumption.

### Gaps
- I did not verify CLARA or other KnowNo follow-ups (for example multi-agent or VLM-based conformal planners), or 2025-2026 "conformal VLA" papers beyond 2604.16677. A dedicated search on "conformal referring expression comprehension" returned nothing directly relevant. Absence of evidence here is weak evidence.

## Q4. Has any robotics paper factorised success into binding x competence and measured calibration?

### Takeaway
Empirical decompositions of VLA failures into grounding vs execution are common in 2026 papers. None I found states success = P(bind) x P(motor | bind) as a model and tests it, or couples the factorisation with calibrated binding probabilities. C1 as an identity is just the chain rule. Its non-trivial content is that motor competence does not depend on which compound or binding is involved, and that is testable.

### Cited Findings
- ProGAL-VLA (2604.09824) reports grounding/grasp/planning failure rates. OpenVLA: 41.2% grounding, 12.5% grasp, 5.1% planning failures, 41.2% success. ProGAL-VLA: 6.3% / 7.1% / 4.5%, 82.1% success. — [arXiv 2604.09824](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09824)
- RoboSPA (2609.05324) separates target-grounding errors (wrong object) from target-execution errors (right object, failed grasp) for π0.5 and X-VLA. — [arXiv 2609.05324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05324)
- In-Context VLA (2608.05738), Table 15, splits failures into perception, grounding and control errors. — [arXiv 2608.05738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05738)
- Training-free action correction (2608.29967): across 133 LIBERO-90 evaluations, "task misunderstanding" (wrong object or relation) dominates at 47% for OFT and 50% for π0.5, vs execution misalignment at 23% / 22%. — [arXiv 2608.29967](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29967v1)
- Guardian (2512.01946) separates planning errors from execution errors at the subplan level. — [arXiv 2512.01946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01946)
- 2603.06001 documents "linguistic blindness" in VLAs on LIBERO: visual priors override instruction semantics. — [arXiv 2603.06001](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06001v1)
- The multiplicative-composition precedent comes from Okawa et al. (2310.09336). — [arXiv 2310.09336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09336)

### Inferences
- **C1 verdict: partial. The decomposition is prior art empirically; the formal factorisation plus its test may be new.** P(success) = P(correct binding) x P(success | correct binding) holds exactly whenever success requires the correct binding. A reviewer will call it the chain rule. Defensible novelty: (i) the testable independence claim that P(success | correct binding, compound) depends only on the motor primitive and not on the compound, (ii) predicting success on unseen compounds from separately measured binding and motor rates, and (iii) calibration, meaning reliability diagrams of the binder's P(bind) against observed binding correctness. None of the papers above does (ii) or (iii).
- **Strongest reviewer objection (C1):** "It is the chain rule, and your failure taxonomy is already in ProGAL-VLA, RoboSPA and 2608.29967." A second objection: in flow VLAs, binding and motor are entangled in one network, so "correct binding" must be defined operationally (for example the first-contact object). That labelling can be circular.

### Gaps
- Calibration of grounding probabilities in VLAs: I found no paper reporting ECE or reliability for target selection. This could also reflect search limits.
- I did not check whether any 2026 paper predicts compound success from atom-level rates (the C1 x C2 combination).
