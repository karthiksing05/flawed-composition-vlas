Counter-evidence rule ("F6_b under W2 below .15, or N1-N6 REVERSED with F6_b above RAA-sL"): neither.  Evidence-for rule ("B1 SUPPORTED with F6_b above .35 AND primary-A not REVERSED AND WK non-inferior"): the first two held, WK did not (COST).  My F6_b point was too low by .6-.7 and my K1-K6 point too high by .3: the mechanism (the expert's xy owns the end of covered transport) was right and bigger than I priced, and the Phase A cost I feared landed on exactly the K cells with elevated targets, far more than I gave it.

**Answer.** Taking the xy away from the expert (W1) turns F6_b from .017 to .817 (W2 .900, +.083 over W1), above frozen pi0.5 (.517) and rgp (.633, W2 CI above 0), equal to rc (.800); D2' becomes SUPPORTED, so the F6_b reversal of SR 82 was the expert's xy, not the channel.  The gate adds a small gain on F6_b and changes nothing else.  The price is paid on tasks with an elevated target (K3 .967 -> 0, K5 .683 -> 0, K4 .983 -> .283, N3 .833 -> .333): K1-K6 falls .41, N1-N6 is not significantly different (-.04 / -.02; N1, N6 rise), and on F7 pool -.09, F6_a -.07, controls .458 (gate fails, so the gated PRIMARY is UNTESTABLE).  So a module-owned where recovers the covered-target case and is not usable as a global replacement; a fix needs the expert to keep xy when the target is elevated (not tested here).
