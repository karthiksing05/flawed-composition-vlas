# Adversarial AC review 2: "Routed by Cues: A Failure Atlas of VLAs on New Pairings"

Reviewer stance: skeptical ICML 2027 area chair, fresh read of `paper/icml2027/main.tex`, `parts/matrix.tex`, `parts/appendix.tex` (2026-11-24 build, 40 pages total).
Line numbers: `M:` = main.tex, `X:` = parts/matrix.tex, `A:` = parts/appendix.tex.
Prior work checked online (arXiv abstract/HTML): CRAFT 2610.00524, ReGuide 2609.38616, Gerigk et al. 2610.10912, ACT-VLA 2607.00351, Jiang et al. 2610.06235, Schmalz & Gupta (NeurIPS 2026 RoboPAD workshop poster page). The paper's citations of these are accurate. One consequence matters below: CRAFT's reported numbers (pi0.5 8.4 -> 84.2%, pi0 2.8 -> 64.3%, GR00T N1.7 1.1 -> 72.1%, Table 1, Pick-Place) come from its "Full" setting, which updates both the VLM and the action expert, on LIBERO-derived benchmarks.

## Score: 4 / 10 (weak reject)

The paper has strengths. The pre-registration is careful, the reporting of failed predictions and deviations is unusually honest, the cue tests are causal, the same-state reference planner gate is a good idea, and the four-seed pi0 study is the cleanest experiment in the paper. Against that, the necessity thesis the authors want to establish ends up stated as "an empirical claim over our recipe" (M:52, M:229). The recipe (frozen VLM, 6k-step action-expert fine-tune) leaves out the one in-network method that is known to work. The pi0.5 evidence is not significant. The "learned owner" evidence is weaker than the text says, because H* is trained to imitate the servo law. The paper is also two papers in one: an atlas, plus a necessity argument for a where-module. It is written in dense internal shorthand with dozens of undefined arm names. Taken together, the paper sits below the ICML bar.

## (a) Three strongest objections, ranked

### 1. The recipe scoping removes the comparison that decides necessity
The claim is that a separate module must own the where, rather than letting the network learn it. The only in-network training that has reached high held-out success on comparable LIBERO-derived splits is CRAFT, which fine-tunes the VLM and the expert on counterfactual pairs that cover the pairings (verified). ACT-VLA, AC-VLA and task scrubbing (Gerigk et al.) are also in-network training methods. The recipe freezes the VLM and trains the expert for 6,001 steps at batch 32, which is roughly 192k samples, or a few passes over 52k frames. On pi0 the "network learns it" arm (A0) must also learn from a 4-d token written over its trained end-effector-position state columns (M:222, A:428). The text therefore does not claim necessity; it claims that a fixed-form owner beats a short, small-capacity in-network fine-tune under one data regime. The paper never tests the obvious alternative explanation, under-training or sample efficiency: no longer schedule, no unfrozen VLM, no auxiliary where-loss, and no token added alongside native state. Without one of those, a reviewer will read d1/n1s as "an inductive prior helps at 6k steps", which is unsurprising, and not as necessity.

### 2. The "learned separate owner" is distilled from the servo, so it does not separate "separation" from "form"
M:204 asks whether "a separate learned module that owns the where, with no hand-chosen form" beats in-network learning, and answers yes on pi0 (+.191 over four seeds). A:890 defines H*'s regression target as `y_j = clip(2.851 (rel(min(t+j,t_r)) - rel(t_r)), ±.9375)`, the servo's linear-saturating law with its fitted gain and its clip, aimed at a learned release point. H* is therefore an MLP approximation of the servo plus a learned release offset. The H that is genuinely learned (E4, fit to demonstration actions) fails its G1 gate (.678) and is n.s. on pi0.5 (+.089 [-.208, +.346]). Consequences:
- n1s (H* - A0) and d1s (sL - A0) are close to the same contrast: two servo-shaped owners against the same A0. They are not independent lines of evidence, and n1s does not support "separation over form" (A:974, M:204).
- A jointly trained dedicated branch, the arm that would separate "a separate pathway" from "a frozen fitted prior", was not run (A:884, item 7: "an end-to-end trained head ... not run").

### 3. The pi0.5 half of the necessity argument is null, and the asymmetric reading of nulls carries the matrix
On pi0.5 the best servo-free route A trails RAA-sL by -.100 [-.261, +.050] (development) and -.086 [-.244, +.056] (new set), both n.s. The comparator is RAA-sL seed 1 (.878). Seed 2 scored .822 (A:408), and against the seed mean (.850) the A gap shrinks to about -.07. Even so, M:215 and X:12 read "training alone did not reach it". "Every repair that worked places an explicit component outside the trained weights" (M:52, M:76, M:215, M:229) depends on two choices. First, "worked" is never defined as a threshold. Second, the input edits (tvpb, painted views) count as explicit components, so almost anything that is not a weight update counts as a win for the thesis. The partial in-network wins (RAA over R2 +.267; A at .778) are read as failures while non-significant gaps favour the thesis. The categorisation should be operational, with a stated threshold, or the sentence should go.

Runners-up: (i) every repair uses privileged perception (rendered depth, simulator masks, scene-2 channel error 0; M:221). The pi0 contrast is information-matched, which is good, but the atlas-to-repair story is not. (ii) LIBERO only, simulation only, two scenes. (iii) The atlas F-entries are mostly on development states, and only F1/F2 replicate on held-out states (M:223).

## (b) Is the necessity claim, as now stated, supported?

**As literally stated, "an empirical claim over our recipe, not a proof", the pi0 half is supported and the pi0.5 half is not. The stated claim is also too weak to be the paper's thesis.**

- **Four-seed pi0 result (A:946-974).** It is robust in direction and size. n1s +.191 [+.107, +.267], positive on all four seeds (+.086 to +.256), with a post hoc paired t = 4.8 on 3 df (I recomputed this from the per-seed values: mean .191, SD .079). The four-seed percentile bootstrap is somewhat anti-conservative, but the t-test agrees. d1s -.227 [-.287, -.164] across seeds replicates d1. Per-seed SD on N is .03-.06, so single-seed policy gaps of about .06 are within noise, as M:223 concedes. What it shows: on pi0 under this recipe, a servo-shaped owner fed the exact target token beats an expert trained on the same token. What it does not show: that a learned-form owner suffices (H* has the servo form), that the gap survives more training or an unfrozen VLM, or anything about pi0.5. n2s -.036 is "within .04 of the servo", but non-inferiority at -.10 is not shown (lower bound -.103). The abstract's "comes within .04" should carry that qualifier.
- **Replications.** The pi0 servo contrast replicates on sets 5, 6 and 7: -.222, -.208, -.227 (four seeds on set 7). This part is solid. The W5/ReGuide parity (.892/.931/.894 vs .914/.883/.917) is descriptive. Non-inferiority is never shown, and two of the three pooled sets were seen before registration (A:232). The removal ladder (r1 +.678) is, by the authors' own pre-registered reading, dependence and not necessity (M:202, A:800). It should not appear in the necessity column at all (X:12 "dependence, not necessity" is right; the conclusion, abstract and contribution 3 should match).
- **Matrix (X:12-18).** Only F1 has both in-network columns filled (M:215, acknowledged). F1m, F2, F6 and F7 have "not tested" for more data and/or removal. So the matrix supports sufficiency of explicit components with privileged inputs, plus one comparative result (pi0). It cannot support "necessary" for any failure except F1-on-pi0.
- **Scoping to a recipe.** It is honest, but it makes the claim close to unfalsifiable within the paper and leaves out exactly the published route that works. CRAFT updates the VLM; this recipe freezes it. A reviewer will ask why the recipe was chosen, and whether the result is a property of the recipe's capacity and budget or of distributed architectures. The paper has no experiment that moves along either axis.

## (c) Overclaims, number inconsistencies, undefined terms

### Overclaims
- M:204: "a separate learned module that owns the where, with no hand-chosen form". This is false given A:890 (the target is the clipped servo law, gain 2.851, clip .9375). Also M:52 "A separate learned owner", A:974 "favours separation over form".
- M:52, M:76, M:215, M:229: "Every repair that worked places an explicit component outside the trained weights". "Worked" is undefined, and input edits are counted as explicit components. See objection 3.
- M:52: "The pi0 servo contrast now replicates on three new held-out sets". "now" is revision language.
- M:75: "with two seeds where available". On development states the second seed gave D2 -.067 n.s. where seed 1 was REVERSED (A:411). The removal-test seeds do not agree on the memory half, which this phrasing hides.
- M:76: "Explicit components ... repair the failures". F2 is lifted only to .608 (pi0, ReGuide) and F1m only "in part" (X:13-14).
- M:89 / M:52: "a where-module inside a retrained policy". W5 is a serving-time module on a residual-trained checkpoint (M:195 "train nothing"). "Inside" is misleading.
- M:222: "the servo-kept share of -.150 is leftover dependence on those columns". This is an untested interpretation (A:521 asserts it).
- M:187: "A rule we fixed before the rollouts holds". The pi0 development-rollout registration landed 7 s after the first job was submitted (A:565). That is disclosed, but it is borderline for "fixed before".

### Number and status inconsistencies
- Seed/set counts are stale in several places. M:52 (end) says "mostly development states (one new held-out set for pi0 and the removal ladder)", but the same abstract claims three new held-out sets. M:76 "On pi0 (one seed)". M:79 Scope "one seed per policy (two for RAA-sL) ... one new held-out set". M:189 "It still rests on one training seed". M:222 "all rest on one training seed". M:223 "the pi0 contrast and the removal ladder also have the new set". All predate the six- and seventh-set studies.
- A:262: "the sealed fifth set, which stays unbuilt" contradicts A:771 ("The fifth set holds 60 new start states ... built and registered") and M:189.
- The Holm family is unstable. M:189 and A:800/A:872 use a 3-primary family (d1' adj p .003). A:929 uses a 4-primary family (d1' adj p .0045, n1 added later). A:949 uses a 2-primary family (n1s, d1s). The abstract (M:52) says "a multiplicity correction only over the three primaries of that set". Report one pre-declared family per study and say that families were added sequentially.
- RAA-sL prefix share: .089 (M:101, A:262, A:303) vs "the prefix carries .132" (M:166). The likely cause is different observation sets (96 N+K vs 124 incl. F7), but the text does not say so.
- M:185: pi0 A0 share .542 minus pi0.5 A0 -.001 is .543 on point estimates, but the text reports +.531 [+.463, +.607]. Explain (paired subset or bootstrap mean) or report the matching point.
- M:111: E = +.450 (excess failure) and the success rate ".450 to .817" (role views) are the same number with different meanings in the same paragraph, followed by the defensive sentence "E is an excess failure rate and is not a success rate". This reads as an error. Rename one or show both with units.
- M:101 vs M:166 vs Table J1 (A:413): three servo shares (.871, .878, .920) with different denominators. Readers will see a contradiction. One table should give all of them with their denominators.
- X:15-16: removal drops have negative sign (-.217, -.483) while Table removal (A:197-199) reports them as positive drops (+.217, +.483). Use one sign convention.
- M:133: "cliff ... from 1.21 at layer 13 to .10 at layer 14, a narrow read site at ... expert layer 14". If "patch at layer L" means the residual input to layer L, the cliff points to layer-13 KV. State the indexing.
- M:52 "the held outline picks the carry" vs M:113 "the held outline starts the carry" (also X:13 "starts", A:104 "picks").
- M:189: "$p = .003$,," (double comma).

### Undefined or colliding terms
- Never expanded: RAA (the central arm family, M:157), tvpb (M:68, M:111), noW (M:202), rgp / rgpr / rc / RC (X:2, A:83, A:683; the case also varies), E4 (M:223), NPHS / nphs (A:949), U and P_min planners (A:17, A:73), "Phase A / Phase B" (A:303, A:528), K1t / K2t (A:83, A:800), in.tok / in.prefix / in.servo / in.chan / abl.tok (A:412-516), S_off / S_kept (A:523), pzA0 / pzsL (A:521), "T" in the fit gate (A:493), "seed-widened" intervals and σ_s (A:893, A:903), "capture subset" (A:549), c_clean / φ_zero / σ_demo (A:442), "home advantage" (M:222), "testability floor" (M:121; defined only at A:13).
- Set numbering: "held-out sets 1/2/3" (W5), "set 4" (A:234, mentioned once), then "fifth/sixth/seventh set". Sets 1-4 are never defined as a sequence. A one-line table of all state sets (name, size, when built, which arms read it) is needed.
- Name collisions: failure class "H" (held at step cap, A:720) vs head "H" (A:833), as in "class-H share of H's failures" (A:931). Criteria "C1/C2/C3" (A:19) vs W5 components "C1/C2" (A:175). Failure class "W" vs where-module "W5".
- "where-share" (M:101), "follow share φ" (M:179), "in.tok share" and "prefix share" are four share statistics. Only the first is defined in the main text.
- "learned where-share" pass rule: the prefix condition is stated as deciding no verdict (M:101). Then drop it from the main text.

## (d) Cheapest changes that would raise the score by about a point

GPU-h estimates scale from the paper's own logs: H* study 18.3 GPU-h (training 8.4, rollouts 9.4; A:942). NPHS 108.4 GPU-h for 12 pi0 arm-seeds including set-7 rollouts (A:972), about 9 GPU-h per arm-seed at 6k steps.

**Text only (0 GPU-h). Required, and worth about half a point on their own.**
1. State the supported claim precisely and lead with it: "Under frozen-VLM, 6k-step expert fine-tuning on LIBERO, a fixed-form owner fed the exact target beats the same expert learning from the same token on pi0 (four seeds, three new sets); on pi0.5 the gap is not significant." Delete "with no hand-chosen form" (M:204). Rename H* as "servo-distilled head with learned release point". Drop "favours separation over form" (A:974).
2. Name in the main text that CRAFT updates the VLM (verified) and that the recipe freezes it, so the scoping is visibly a choice and not a hidden exclusion. Give a reason for the recipe.
3. Fix every stale count (M:52 end, M:76, M:79, M:189, M:222, M:223), the "unbuilt fifth set" (A:262), the Holm families, and the .089/.132 and +.531 points. Add a state-set table and a glossary of arm names, or rename arms descriptively (e.g. "pi0-noServo", "pi0-servo", "pi0-head").
4. Define "a repair worked" with a threshold (for example, CI lower bound within -.10 of ReGuide or the reference planner) and apply it to every matrix cell, including RAA and A.
5. Focus. Either title the paper as an atlas and demote necessity to one hedged section, or make necessity the thesis and move the atlas to supporting evidence. At present each half dilutes the other. Confirm that the body fits the 8-page limit; it is dense, with 4 full-width floats plus the matrix.

**Experiments (ranked by value per GPU-h)**
6. **Training-budget / capacity control for the pi0 in-network arm, about 45-60 GPU-h.** pi0-A0 at 4x steps (24k), 2 seeds, set-7 rollouts (reuse the existing sL/H* set-7 numbers as comparators): about 2 x (17 training + 5 rollout) = 45 GPU-h. Optionally add one seed with the VLM unfrozen (LoRA): about 15-20 GPU-h more. If the gap persists, necessity becomes a real finding at the paper's scale. If it closes, the paper has an honest sample-efficiency result. Either outcome removes objection 1's under-training reading. This is the single largest lever.
7. **Jointly trained dedicated where-branch (in-network, structured, not frozen, no servo target), 4 seeds on set 7, about 36-40 GPU-h.** The H* architecture as a residual branch trained end-to-end with the expert, or A0 plus an auxiliary loss predicting displacement-to-release. This separates "separate pathway / architectural prior" from "frozen fitted servo", which is the authors' stated question (objection 2).
8. **Token alongside native state on pi0 (no overwrite of dims 0-3), A0 and sL, 2 seeds, about 36 GPU-h.** This removes the "A0 lost proprioception" confound (M:222). Cheaper variant: A0 only, 2 seeds, about 18 GPU-h.
9. **Learned-form head on pi0: H** fit to demonstration displacement-to-release without the servo's gain or clip, 4 seeds, about 36 GPU-h.** This is the actual test of "separation, not form", which E4's H could not answer (gate failure, single seed, pi0.5).
10. **Settle pi0.5: 3 more seeds each of A and RAA-sL on set 7, about 55 GPU-h.** This would either make the pi0.5 gap significant or let the paper state cleanly that pi0.5 shows no necessity.

Minimum package for +1 point: items 1-5 (text) plus 6 (about 45 GPU-h). Items 7 and 9 together (about 75 GPU-h) are what would make the necessity claim worth the paper's thesis.
