# SR 88 diagnostic: why W5 loses N4, N6, K6, K1, F6_b and the unique-name controls (EXPLORATORY, not registered, no new arm, nothing implemented)

Written 2026-11-20 after SR 87.  The SR 87 states (Phase A trials 0-59 = k 580-639, Phase B rgc k 384-444 / 504-563) are now DEVELOPMENT states; none of the numbers here is a test.
No label is attached to any number.  One checkpoint (pi05_g8v_raas_l_ae main/6000), one seed, one trial per state.

## Method
**Instrumented replays.**  The SR 87 rollouts logged the channel error and the server's per-call where log (no episode ids, no simulator track in Phase A), so the cells were re-run with an observer around the LIBERO env
(`experiments/sr88_instr.py` = copy of sr86_instr.py; `raa` mode for the N / K cells via raa.py + raa_w.py, `wa` mode for the rgc cells via wa_sw5.py; adds `ep_start` markers to the server's where / chan logs).  It only reads sim.data after each step.
Per episode: executed action (gripper command), EE and object xyz, target xyz, the On-terms, named contacts; per 5-step call: object / EE, channel rel vs truth, mu class, ownership (own), gate, c, e = |rel - mu|, expert and module xy, gripper command before / after the gate.  Joined by `experiments/sr88_read.py`.
**The replays are exact**: for every (cell, arm, state) the re-run outcome equals the SR 87 outcome (480 episode comparisons, 0 disagreements; `results/flawed/sr88/read_tables.txt`, last section).  So "SR 87 loss" and "replay loss" are the same events, and a replay is a causal handle on the same trajectory.
**States** (rule fixed before the replays, `experiments/sr88_mkbr.py`, `results/flawed/sr88/sel.json`): 20 per cell = W5 failures where W4 or W5c1 (uniL: or RAA-sL) succeeded (at most 14), then all-fail states (at most 4), then states all three succeeded.  Rates below are therefore NOT estimates of the cell rates (selected for W5 failing); they are for paired mechanism comparison.
Cells: N4 (tid 22, bottle -> plate beside the cabinet), N6 (tid 26, bottle -> bowl), K6 (tid 10, cream cheese -> bowl), K1 (tid 0, bowl -> plate), F6_b (rgc tid 8, bowl -> displaced plate), uniL (rgc tid 4, "the bowl", second bowl hidden).  Arms W5 / W4 / W5c1 / RAA-sL, 8 GPU jobs (4813373-80), 480 episodes.
Jobs: picks 4813365, branch subsets 4813369, replays 4813373 (W5 A) 4813374 (W5 B) 4813375 (W4 A) 4813376 (W4 B) 4813377 (W5c1 A) 4813378 (W5c1 B) 4813379 (RAA-sL A) 4813380 (RAA-sL B), CPU reads 4813398-4813625 (final table = 4813625, copied to `results/flawed/sr88/read_tables.txt`), held-error table 4813588 (`experiments/sr88_src.py`), channel peeks 4813585 / 4813601 / 4813632 / 4813635 / 4813637.
Per-call timelines of every episode (W5 beside W4, W5c1, RAA-sL): `results/flawed/sr88/timelines_<cell>.txt`; per-episode summaries `results/flawed/sr88/read_eps.json`.

## Gate radius (2 -> 3 cm) versus C2 proper
The 2 -> 3 cm release gate and the hand-off radius cannot be separated by an arm here (W5c2 was not replayed and there is no "3 cm hand-off with a 2 cm gate" arm); what the logs show:
- Within W5 the first release lands at a channel |rel_xy| < 2 cm (n 43) or 2-3 cm (n 28, the band W4's gate would have blocked): episode success **.30 vs .29**.  The band the gate change opened does not release worse than the band W4 would also allow.  W4 / W5c1 releases below 2 cm succeed .71 / .73, RAA-sL .89.
- The failures in W5's < 2 cm band have a body-frame release offset >= 3 cm in 56% (W4 42%, W5c1 34%, RAA-sL 25%): the object keeps moving between the call that said "inside 2 cm" and the release row (overshoot, above), i.e. the hand-off, not the gate, produces the offset.
- Gate calls per episode are similar (K6 W5 1.1, W4 1.45, W5c1 1.3; K1 .1 / 1.1 / 1.25; uniL .5 / 1.05 / .9) except N6 (W5 8.9, W4 3.3, W5c1 1.6), where the gate holds the gripper closed through a stall that has another cause (below).
- Only K6 shows a gate-sized effect: 7 of 10 W5 K6 failures released at a channel |rel_xy| > 2 cm (6 of them in the 2-3 cm band; success in that band .33 vs .67 below 2 cm, n 9 / 9); W4 / W5c1 / RAA-sL release there at a median 1.1 / 1.25 / 0.8 cm body offset.
Reading: the gate change is at most a secondary contributor on K6 and no contributor elsewhere; the loss is the hand-off.  A direct test needs one more arm (W5 with a 2 cm gate), which this note does not run.

## Per-cell causes (W5 failures replayed; W4 / W5c1 / RAA-sL on the same states)

| cell (replay, selected states) | W5 | W4 | W5c1 | RAA-sL | W5 failure categories (20 states) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N4 | .15 | .80 | .55 | .55 | no_release 11, release_far 6, success 3 |
| N6 | .30 | .80 | .75 | .85 | no_release 6, release_far 4, release_near_fail 2, no_carry 2, success 6 |
| K6 | .50 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | release_far 7, release_near_fail 3, success 10 |
| K1 | .75 | .90 | .90 | 1.00 | release_far 4, release_near_fail 1 |
| F6_b | .30 | .75 | .80 | .00 | release_far 9, release_near_fail 2, no_carry 3, success 6 |
| uniL | .25 | .35 | .35 | .95 | release_far 14, release_near_fail 1, success 5 |
(no_carry = the object never lifted; independent of W5.  "release_far" = first release with the body-origin xy offset to the target >= 3 cm.)

**N4 (bottle -> plate; the plate sits beside the cabinet): hand-off while the bottle is still 11-12 cm above the plate.**  Hand-off height 11.7 cm (W5 fails), the expert owns the whole descent.  After hand-off the true error leaves R in 65% (median max 4.1 cm), and the arm presses the cabinet (`right_gripper>wooden_cabinet_1_g18`) for >= 10 steps in 65% of W5 failures (median 75 steps) vs 6% (W4 ok), 9% (W5c1 ok), 18% (RAA-sL ok).  11 of 17 failures never release (the arm is jammed against the cabinet, gate quiet).  W4 and W5c1 keep the module on xy through the descent (own calls 7 vs 5), and the object descends over the plate.  Demos release a plate target at c about 0, so the hand-off is 11 cm earlier than the demos' own release.  mu is not involved: |mu_plate| = 0.5 cm, and W5 - W5c2 is 0 in SR 87.
**N6 (bottle -> bowl): the channel breaks right after the hand-off and stays broken.**  12 of 14 W5 failures have a held-phase channel error > 5 cm starting a median 3 calls after the hand-off and lasting a median 37 calls (W5 successes 0 of 6; W4 failures 2 of 4 and W5c1 failures 5 of 5 but late, first call 34-36 = after the stall; RAA-sL ok 5 of 17 for 1-2 calls).  Example t44 / t58: in one call the target rel jumps from (0, +1, -7) to (+7, +1, +11) (true (-1, +1, -5)): error +7.9 cm in x and +16 cm in z, source `agent` then frozen as `occl`; the expert then reads "target 11 cm above" and the object rises / hovers.  6 of 14 never release; the gate fires in 95% of W5 episodes of the cell (mean 8.9 calls vs W4 3.3, W5c1 1.6); true e leaves R in 83% and the module re-owns xy in 83% (chatter at the radius).  Onset: the held bottle is 5 cm above the bowl at xy 1-3 cm, i.e. directly over it.  The same single-call jump occurs in RAA-sL (5 of 17 ok episodes) and in W4 (4 of 16) but is transient there (1-2 calls, the object keeps descending and releases); W5's expert owns the descent and dwells above the bowl.  Which entity's estimate jumps (object or target) is not separable with these logs (combined axis error).
**K6 (cream cheese -> bowl) and K1 (bowl -> plate): hand-off with momentum, the expert does not take the residual out.**  Crossing speed 5.8-6.4 cm per chunk vs 2.8 for RAA-sL (4.1 K1), overshoot 2.6 cm (W5 K6 fails; 1.1 ok; RAA-sL 0.0), release 3.0-4.3 cm (K6 W5 fails; all 7 release_far: 3.1-4.3, the near_fail 2.9-3.0) vs 0.1-1.9 for W4 / W5c1 / RAA-sL on the same 10 states.  Example K6 t25: e falls 6.4 -> 1.2 cm in one chunk (hand-off at c 9.5 cm), body offset then 1.7 -> 3.0 -> 3.3 -> 4.1 -> 5.1 cm over the next four calls as the gripper opens (the object drifts with the momentum the module gave it; W4 keeps xy on the module through the same calls and stays at 1.2-2.6 cm).  Module re-owns xy after hand-off in 90% of K6 failures.  K1: 3 of the 5 W5 failures (t05, t11, t44) are this; t16 fails for every module arm (released 12 cm above the plate, 8.6 cm off) and RAA-sL succeeds: a carry failure unrelated to C2; t57 fails for W5c1 too.
**F6_b (bowl -> displaced plate): hand-off radius equals the success tolerance, and the expert has no model of the displaced plate to take the residual out.**  W5 hands off at e = 1.2-3.6 cm (true, channel frame) and the release follows at 2.9-4.6 cm (9 of 11 releases >= 3 cm; on the same 10 paired states W4 succeeds on 9 and W5c1 on 10, with releases at 0.8-3.8 cm); overshoot is 0.0 (not momentum).  RAA-sL alone releases 5.9 cm off (it goes to the trained plate position: success .07), so inside R the expert is not an aligner for this cell: nothing nulls the 2-3 cm left at hand-off.  Release height (body dz) 2.7 cm W5 vs 0.9 cm W4.
**uniL (unique-name control): the held-object estimate is coarse and grows during module-driven carries.**  Held-object error at the onset of the carry is 1.9 cm (mean, identical for every arm: the same pre-grasp path), then grows to a mean per-episode maximum of **5.1 cm (W5), 4.8 (W5c2), 5.8 (W4), 6.2 (W5c1) vs 2.5 (RAA-sL)** (60 states per arm, SR 87 rollouts).  63% of W5 episodes have a median held error >= 2 cm (F7 relL 32%, relR 3%, F6_b 8%, F6_a 13%); success in that bin: W5 .55, W5c2 .50, W4 .26, W5c1 .32, RAA-sL .89; below 2 cm the arms are .90-1.00.  The module nulls a wrong rel (failed W5 uniL episodes end with the channel's true rel at (-1.0, +1.0) cm vs mu and a frame offset (channel vs body) of +1.9 cm in y vs +0.1 in successes), so the body offset at release is the error: median 4.0 cm (W5) vs 2.9 (RAA-sL).  C2 is a gain here (W5c2 - W4 +.18 in SR 87 on uniL), not a loss; the remaining gap to RAA-sL (.683 vs .933) is the module's estimate and the speed it drives the object at (error growth is the swing / slip of the held bowl), not the hand-off.  The held-object source is `agent` (a measurement) in every held query, so this is not the pose memory.

## Proposed changes (ONE per cause; none implemented, none tuned on these cells; every constant comes from the training demos or from a measured module error)
All keep "module owns WHERE, expert owns HOW".

1. **N4 (and the early part of N6): the module owns xy down to the demos' release height (hand-off by phase, not by radius alone).**  Hand-off requires |e| <= R AND c <= c_rel(class), where c_rel(class) = the median over the training demos of the channel height c = -dz at the first open command, from the same demo pass that gives mu (SR 86 table: plate about 0, bowl 6.9 cm, stove 2.3, cabinet 0, rack -2.2; computed once, committed before any rollout, unknown class: the current CLEAR 3 cm).  WHERE includes the path to the release point; the expert's HOW (release timing, final z) starts where the demos' own release is.  Evidence for: W5 success falls monotonically with hand-off height (.70 / .54 / .27 / .14), N4 jams on the cabinet only in the 11.7 cm hand-offs, W4 (module to c < 3 cm) is .80 on the same states.  Prediction to register if run: N4 back to W4's level; K6 / N6 hand-offs move 2-3 cm lower; F7 / controls (where C2 helped) unchanged because their hand-offs sit near c_rel already.
2. **K6, K1, uniL (and the 2.6-3.9 cm overshoots in N6 / N4): the module drives with the demos' own approach-speed profile, not at saturation.**  Replace the saturating servo (K_w = servo_max / 2 cm, max = the demos' p99 clip) by v_cmd(e) = the median demo xy speed at channel distance e from the class release point (profile measured once on the training demos, bins over 0-20 cm, no fitted constant).  The module still supplies only the aim (WHERE); speed is HOW and comes from the demos, so the expert receives the object at the speed it was trained on and the module cannot swing the held object (uniL error growth 5-6 cm vs 2.5 for the expert's own carry).  Evidence for: crossing speed 5.8-6.4 vs 2.8-4.1 cm per chunk (module vs expert), overshoot 2.6-3.9 vs 0.0-0.5 in K6 (RAA-sL), success vs overshoot .24 (>= 2.5 cm) vs .44-.50.
3. **F6_b (and the residual part of K6 / K1): hand-off radius from the expert's own placement scatter, not from the success tolerance.**  R_h(class) = the median over the training demos of |release rel - mu(class)| (the demos' own xy scatter around the release point, computed in the same pass as mu), instead of R = 3 cm = the LIBERO body-target tolerance; inside R_h the expert owns xy, outside the module does.  Evidence for: handing over at the tolerance leaves the expert up to 3 cm of residual that, for F6_b, it cannot take out (RAA-sL alone releases 5.9 cm off; W5 releases at 2.9-4.6 cm with hand-off e 1.7-3.6), and success rises weakly as hand-off e falls (.48 below 1.5 cm vs .33-.35 above).  This is the weakest of the five: the pooled gradient is small and R_h per class is not yet measured.
4. **N6: hold the target's pose from before the carry (the target is static unless pushed).**  The module's target estimate is the last measurement taken while the target was unoccluded by the held object; a later measurement replaces it only if it differs from it by less than the p99 frame-to-frame change of that estimate over the training demos (measured, not fitted); otherwise the held value stays (this is the pose memory M applied to a measurement that jumps, not only to an empty mask).  Evidence for: 12 / 14 W5 N6 failures start with one-call jumps of 8 cm (x) and 16 cm (z) as the bottle covers the bowl and then persist (37 calls); with the held pose the expert would see "target 0 cm below" instead of "11 cm above".  Not tried; the logs do not say whether the object or the target estimate jumps.
5. **uniL / unique-name controls: nothing new beyond 2.**  The loss against RAA-sL is the module's coarse held-object estimate (>= 2 cm in 63% of episodes, 1.9 cm at the onset) plus its growth; proposal 2 attacks the growth (same principle: the module does not drive faster than the demos), the onset error is the perception module's (pose at grasp), outside this note.

## Limits
Selection (14 W5 losers + controls per cell, W5 failing) makes every rate above a paired-mechanism statistic, not a cell rate; 20 states per cell; the replays are exact, so there is no noise to average, only 20 trajectories per cell; the causes are observational (logs, contacts, paired arms on identical states), no intervention was run, so none of the five proposals is tested;
the release row is the first open command after a lifted closed carry (successes often end before any open command: 23 (W5), 46 (W4), 38 (W5c1), 38 (RAA-sL) of the successes have no release row and their offset is taken at the end); channel rel and truth are the logs' integer centimetres; 5-step overshoot is measured along the approach direction of the chunk before the crossing;
the gate-radius separation is by release band inside W5 (an association, not an arm); the N6 jump cannot be assigned to the object or the target estimate; F6_b cause 3 rests on 10 paired states; obstacle contacts count robot bodies touching anything but the target / object (N4: the cabinet).
