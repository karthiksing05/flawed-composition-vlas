# Measurements stay novel, mechanisms need credit

As of 27 October 2026, vla-operator-binding is novel mainly in what it **measures** and in how it **puts known pieces together**. Almost none of its mechanisms is new on its own. **(A) The failure atlas is the most defensible contribution.** None of the 17 compositional robot benchmarks read grades VLA binding error by referring-expression depth, and none uses Keysers-style compound divergence (CD) at fixed atom divergence. No published fix has been stratified along either axis. The LIBERO left/right mirror is unreported, but the pieces needed to find it are public, so it belongs in the paper as a caveat-grade finding, not a headline. **(B) The theory is mostly prior art in substance.** success = P(binding) x competence is ReGuide's beta x gamma. "Compositional iff invariant across layouts" restates IRM/ICP with layouts as the environments. The identifiability argument is the cow/camel point that motivates IRM, and Wiedemer et al.'s compositional-support condition. Multiplicativity is Okawa et al. and Dziri et al. The shape-bias parallel is Kemp, Perfors and Tenenbaum's variability overhypothesis. Two pieces survive: the *leave-layout-out* (transfer-scored) prequential MDL criterion, and the use of the factorisation as a falsifiable *pre-rollout prediction*. Both are close to standard tools, namely leave-one-environment-out model selection and Elmoznino et al.'s prequential compositionality. **(C) The frozen-VLA crutch is novel as a system, but its parts are prior art.** **(D) BindVLA is novel as a conjunction.** RoboGround, POT-VLA, Pelican-VLA 0.5 and OTTER each overlap one of its five components. Two components had no hits in any VLA paper: two-hop "A of B" resolution and a V-REx penalty across layouts. Both are applications of known ideas. The project's own pre-registered results (B1, B1.1, B1.2) also undercut two theory predictions: atom invariance is rejected, and holistic error *falls* with CD on the current units. The paper therefore has to present the theory as tested and partly refuted, or fix the split before claiming it. Evidence quality is uneven. Many key papers were read in full HTML, but through a summarising fetch tool. Others were read as abstracts or snippets only, or recalled from prior knowledge. ICLR 2027 OpenReview submissions were not searched. Every "novel" verdict is therefore provisional.

## Claim A: the depth and divergence axes are open, the headline is not

### No robot benchmark measures compound divergence or referring depth

The compositional-evaluation literature for robots has grown quickly in 2026, and every benchmark found uses categorical held-out splits. **ATOM-Bench** has 12 held-out composition tasks per platform and does not mention MCD. It reports **pi0.5 at 83.3% atomic score but 15.8% compositional success** ([ATOM-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16826), full text). **RoboCombine** splits into seen / partial-overlap / unseen sets and gets **40.0 / 15.0 / 4.4%** ordered success ([RoboCombine](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15425), full text; NeurIPS venue unconfirmed). **Spa-Bench** comes closest. Its withheld cells keep "each withheld cell's components" in training, which preserves atoms. It never measures or grades CD, and it has no depth or sub-region factor. OOD success is **52.0 / 44.3 / 40.7%** against 81.7–89.2% on matched direct instructions ([Spa-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12696), full text). "Diagnosing Compositional Generalization in Sequential Robot Tasks" uses pair-coverage sets, with OOD success rising **0.83 → 23.93 → 82.29%** as the budget goes from 4 to 16. Its Prop. 4.1 bounds the generalisation gap by an "instruction-compositional shift" term, but it never estimates that term, and it uses a custom DINOv2 flow policy, not a pretrained VLA ([2607.29687](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29687), full text). "Scale Up Strategically" is the only robot paper that cites Keysers et al., and only in related work. It trains on a diagonal of correlated factors and tests off-diagonal, scoring factor dominance post hoc ([2607.21582](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21582), partial full text). SmolVLA, the BindVLA base, is the cleanest motivating datapoint: **44% success on in-distribution object–region pairings, 0% on held-out pairings**, with every atom seen ([2602.24143](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.24143), full text). The only depth-stratified study found is VLM-only. DeepSeek-VL2 falls from **41.1 to 11.1** as spatial relations go from 0 to 3, while a compositional MGA-Net stays flat ([2511.06146](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06146), full text). That paper should be cited as the VLM precedent that the atlas extends to closed-loop action. RefSpatial-Bench has items needing up to 5 reasoning steps but does not report by step count, and it evaluates pointers, not VLAs ([RoboRefer](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04308), partial full text).

The qualitative headline, that VLAs keep their skills and bind the wrong referent, is crowded. ReGuide writes **Pr(S_k) = beta_k x gamma_k** and reports bare-policy composition averages of 8.5 / 39.0 / 20.6 / 13.0 for pi0 / pi0.5 / X-VLA / GR00T N1.7 ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616), full text). "When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories" finds **69% of 381 pi0.5 probes** follow a familiar behaviour ([2609.39971](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39971), full text). "What Can One Sentence Fix?" attributes about **47% (OFT) and 50% (pi0.5)** of 133 failing LIBERO-90 pairs to task misunderstanding, mainly "a relative spatial reference in the image frame rather than the table frame" ([2608.29967](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29967), full text). The atlas must credit these and claim only the new axes. The project's own E4 gradient fits that framing: binding error **0.18 / 0.38 / 0.945** for type / instance / sub-region (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §0, internal). No published fix reports per-depth numbers, among them ReGuide, CRAFT ([2610.00524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00524)), CAG ([2602.17659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17659)) and Pointing-VLA ([2608.23138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23138)). The claim that fixes degrade with depth must therefore come from new experiments. ReGuide's own table hints at it: bare pi0.5 scores 5/50 on "the open top drawer" against 42/50 on the bowl variant ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)).

The strongest objection is confounding. Deeper references need smaller, articulated, rarer targets and more entities. High-CD splits put rarer co-occurrences into training. And 2607.29687 already shows that pair coverage explains OOD success, so MCD binning may only re-parameterise coverage. Three answers work together. First, match scenes across depth strata, so the same frame carries type, instance and sub-region references, with referent area and training frequency held fixed. Second, report CD alongside 2607.29687-style pair coverage and show that CD adds predictive power. Third, report beta and gamma per depth, so the gradient is shown to sit in binding and not in motor competence. The project's own data add a warning. On the 11 recombination dev tasks, **CD is confounded with task type** (the 0.5 bin holds type-level tasks, the 0.1 bin instance qualifiers), and every binder's error *falls* with CD, B1b's included (slope −0.87, CI [−1.44, −0.11]) (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §8, §12, internal). One 2026 Transformer study also reports "the more compound divergence the better" in its setting ([2609.13144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13144), snippet only). The CD axis therefore needs a purpose-built split with CD varied *within* a task family before the paper can claim a direction.

### The LIBERO mirror is unreported but one step from public material

Each ingredient is documented. openpi rotates agentview 180° (`[::-1, ::-1]`) "to match train preprocessing" ([openpi main.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/main/examples/libero/main.py)). Show-Harness PR #4 states that a vertical flip and a 180° rotation "differ by a horizontal mirror", with world +y moving to opposite sides of the screen. It never mentions instruction words ([Show-Harness PR #4](https://github.com/showlab/Show-Harness/pull/4)). The LIBERO, openpi and lerobot issue trackers show vertical-flip reports only ([LIBERO issue search](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO+flip)). Spa-Bench treats the frame as a design choice and defines all directions "from the robot's perspective" ([Spa-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12696)). The project's check goes further than any of this. In every training frame with a lateral word, the named instance is the image-opposite one: "left bowl" 16/16, "left plate" 32/32, "book on the right" 24/24. Front/back agree with the image in 96/96 frames. Rescoring zero-shot VLMs under the mirrored convention lifts held-out depth 1 from **0.00 to 0.60–0.667**, and left/right units to 0.90–1.00 (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §6.1, internal). The verdict is **appears unreported, moderate confidence**. The GitHub searches were partly rate-limited, and Discord and OpenReview were not searched. The best framing explains the cause behind 2608.29967's "image frame rather than table frame" symptom, and warns that every zero-shot pointer evaluated on LIBERO lateral words is scored against a mirrored convention. A reviewer will say it is a preprocessing artefact, not a scientific finding. The defence is its consequence: published LIBERO grounding numbers for zero-shot binders are biased on lateral words, and fitted binders silently learn the convention, as B1.1 and B1.2's DINOv2 "left" prototypes did. Before claiming it, confirm against the LIBERO paper or BDDL documentation which frame "left" was meant in.

## Claim B: five pillars, of which two carry new content

### The factorisation is borrowed, so its value is as a prediction

success = P(binding) x competence is ReGuide's Eq. 1, and it is the chain rule whenever success requires the correct binding ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). What is unclaimed is its use **before rollout**. The field's message runs the other way. ATOM-Bench treats atomic score as a ceiling that does not forecast compositional success, and RoboCombine finds that "high first-stage completion does not reliably convert into downstream success" ([ATOM-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16826); [RoboCombine](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15425)). The only pre-screening work found uses solo-success probes of SAC modules in robosuite. Its pooled r = 0.94 is self-described as descriptive, and the selector's advantage is not significant (McNemar p = 0.25) ([2604.26689](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26689), partial full text). A positive pre-rollout prediction for VLAs would therefore stand out. The objection is "a product of independent probabilities is trivially true under independence". The answer is to state independence (competence given a correct binding does not depend on the compound) as the testable assumption. Then beat the naïve baseline "product of atomic success rates" on unseen compounds, using E2b's calibration (slope 0.997) and S0's oracle-binding repair (0.234 → 0.048) as the measured terms (docs/THEORY_FRAMING.md P1, internal).

### Invariance and identifiability restate IRM unless the criterion is the contribution

The closest compositional-generalisation theory never uses environments. Wiedemer et al. define compositional and sufficient support and note that "the compositional representation of a function f is also not unique". They never state an observational-equivalence or non-identifiability result, and invariance, ICP and IRM do not appear ([Wiedemer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05596), full text). Compositional Risk Minimization assumes p(x|z) invariant from train to test as a modelling assumption. It treats IRM only as a baseline that "require[s] a large diversity in terms of the environments" ([CRM](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06303v3), partial full text). Lippl and Stachenfeld name "memorization leak and shortcut bias" in kernel models, the nearest neighbour to the layout shortcut, without invariance ([2405.16391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16391), abstract). The *mapping* is therefore unclaimed: an atom expert is compositional iff its referent likelihood is invariant across layouts, with layouts as environments. The *mathematics* is not. IRM's cow/camel background example is structurally "a layout code predicts the referent" ([IRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02893), abstract plus prior knowledge). ICP identification needs sufficiently diverse interventions ([ICP for Block MDPs](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/zhang20t/zhang20t.pdf), snippet). Grounding-specific versions of the confound exist too. An image-only model reaches **71.2%** top-2 referring precision without seeing the expression ([Cirik et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1805.11818), snippet). Deconfounded Visual Grounding names "spurious correlations between certain language pattern and object location" ([2112.15324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.15324), snippet). Verdicts: **invariance mapping partial; identifiability prior art in substance.** The defensible novelty is stating the equivalence as a theorem *for binding codes* (layout vs appearance) and tying it to the selection criterion.

The transfer-scored criterion is the pillar with the most content. Prequential MDL for structure selection is established, for example in Bornschein et al.'s causal-structure learning ([2107.05481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05481), abstract). Elmoznino, Jiralerspong, Bengio and Lajoie already **define compositionality as C(Z) = K(Z)/K(Z|W)** and estimate it with prequential coding. In the first 100k of 188k characters read, they score compression on given data, with no leave-context-out ordering ([Elmoznino et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14817), partial full text). No use of *leave-layout-out* prequential MDL to choose word-meaning channels was found. The project has a clean empirical motivation for exactly that gap. B1.2's identity MDL, scored on training layouts, moved most nouns into a 16-parameter layout code, and held-out depth-0 binding fell from **0.860 to 0.300** (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §12, internal). That is the identifiability theorem playing out: a fit-based criterion picked the shortcut its features allowed. Verdict: **likely novel as a criterion for compositional binding**. Reviewers will call it "leave-one-environment-out cross-validation", i.e. DomainBed's training-domain model selection (2007.01434, prior knowledge), and "Elmoznino with a different ordering". The defence is to claim necessity and sufficiency, not the tool. Ordering by context is *necessary*, because i.i.d. prequential MDL inherits the observational equivalence. It is *sufficient* under compositional support. Both halves should be shown empirically against i.i.d. prequential MDL and plain leave-layout-out likelihood.

### Shape bias and multiplicativity are honest parallels, not claims

Kemp, Perfors and Tenenbaum explain the shape bias as a learned overhypothesis about feature variability: shape is homogeneous within a category, colour and texture vary ([Kemp et al. 2007](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/courses/readings/KempPerforsTenenbaum2007_LearningOverhypotheses.pdf), abstract; full text not extracted). That is a variability-invariance account in all but name. Ritter et al. find shape-bias magnitude "varies greatly among architecturally identical, but differently seeded models" ([1706.08606](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.08606), snippet). That is consistent with near-equivalent solutions on the training data. Verdict: **prior art in substance**. The parallel survives as a cognitive framing with one sharp, testable prediction. Matched-colour training (no non-shape variation) should yield no shape bias, and the analogue is one layout per word yielding a layout code (THEORY_FRAMING prediction 5). Check whether Kemp et al. already state this before claiming it. Multiplicativity is likewise prior. Okawa et al. find composition emerging from "multiplicative reliance on the performance of constituent tasks" ([2310.09336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09336), abstract). Dziri et al. argue success decays with computation depth (2305.18654, prior knowledge). CoT Referring defines hop-level complexity for grounding without a product model ([2510.06243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06243), snippet). Verdict: **partial**. The referring-depth instantiation is new, and it only matters as a zero-free-parameter prediction: depth-1 atom accuracies predict depth-k success. The project's data currently cut against it. B1.2's union bound is violated at depths 0 and 1 in every arm (siglip depth 0: observed 0.407 vs bound 0.25, 37/140 units). Atom invariance is rejected for role, "black", "bowl" and lateral words across B1, B1.1 and B1.2 (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §8, §10, §12, internal). The paper should report that refutation as evidence that these binders are not factorised in error. Construction makes the scorer factorised, not the errors independent.

## Claim C: the crutch is a new assembly of named ancestors

The unsupervised prototype binder draws on several lines of prior work. Taniguchi, Taniguchi and Cangelosi infer each word's single channel by Gibbs sampling over per-channel GMMs and reach about 0.99 channel accuracy on an iCub ([Frontiers 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2017.00066/full)). SPARC extracts closed-gripper intervals to find the manipulated object and goal ([2606.13497](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13497)). Robo2VLM segments Open-X by gripper phase ([2505.15517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15517)). Amir et al. co-segment parts from DINO features ([2112.05814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.05814)). G3 composes grounding as a product of factors ([1712.01097](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01097)). None of them learns a lexicon from contact events and plugs it into a frozen flow VLA. The deep image-token K/V adapter has crowded neighbours. 2609.39971 swaps the *whole* prefix cache and drives source completion from 200/200 to 0 ([2609.39971](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39971)). GSR injects frozen-T5 language K/V into action blocks and retrains the head ([2608.02497](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02497)). SPECTRA-VLA adds mask-weighted residuals to post-projector tokens with no closed-loop result ([2610.19651](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19651v1)). TwinBrainVLA feeds stop-gradient generalist K/V ([2601.14133](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14133)). VAP tints SAM2 masks for frozen pi0/pi0.5, lifting real pi0.5 selection from **30% to 80%** ([2512.20014](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20014)). Verdict: **novel as a system, components prior art, adapter partial.**

The strongest objection is "2010-era grounded word learning with DINOv2 features, beaten by Grounded-SAM". The project's results both help and hurt here. From grasp/release events alone, B1 is the best type-level binder on the dev units (depth 0 **0.929** vs GroundingDINO 0.786, Qwen 0.879). B1.1's position-free DINOv2 arm is the first annotation-free binder to pass the seen sub-region bar (0.75). But B1's held-out lateral pass was location memory, and removing linear position dropped :60 from 1.00 to 0.00. No arm passes all three bars (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §8, §10, internal). B0 also closed one route. The pi05_base backbone emits captions and never a `<loc>` token, so "the pretrained backbone still binds" cannot be tested with the weights on disk (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §6, internal). The crutch should therefore be claimed as an annotation-free existence result at depth 0 and on seen sub-regions, together with an explicit negative result on relations. It should not be presented as a solved binder.

## Claim D: BindVLA is a conjunction whose parts each have a rival

Each of the five BindVLA components collides with a different paper. **Phrase-only role queries** meet RoboGround first. RoboGround already has separate object and placement query sets (9 each) with mask-guided attention, but its queries are learnable vectors, its masks come from a supervised GLaMM, and its policy also sees the global CLS and text. It lifts Spatial-instruction success from 16.3 to 33.5 ([RoboGround](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21530), full text). POT-VLA has role-typed TARGET/DESTINATION slots with learned role embeddings and base-frame relation features in GR00T N1.7. Its grounding comes from a planner plus SAM3, with no bottleneck, and it reaches **39/80 → 71/80** on a humanoid ([POT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18016), full text). STORM initialises slots from CLIP embeddings of spaCy-parsed nouns but gives them absolute centre-of-mass position ([STORM](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20381), full text). CTRL-O makes slots controllable by referring expressions without mask supervision ([2503.21747](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21747v1), snippet). Verdict on (1): **partial**. Building each query only from its own phrase tokens, with cross-role attention masked and no binding supervision, is unclaimed.

**Two-hop "A of B" resolution** had no VLA hit. Modular relational grounding is old, though: MAttNet (1801.08186) and INGRESS are cited from snippets in the earlier notes, and CoT Referring defines hop-level complexity ([2510.06243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06243)). Verdict: **novel inside a VLA, prior art as an idea**. The project itself raises a reviewer hook. Head composition lost the training selection to flat composition in B1, B1.1 and B1.2, so no parent was ever resolved (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §7, §9, §11, internal). BindVLA must show its two-hop path is used, by ablating it on depth-2 units.

**Position-free keys with a relative-geometry channel** inherit a long lineage. Act3D and 3D Diffuser Actor use relative 3D rotary attention because the coordinate system is arbitrary ([Act3D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.17817); [3DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10885v2), snippets). Invariant Slot Attention uses slot-relative frames ([2302.04973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04973), abstract). Role-stream-keys and filler-stream-values give systematicity in Chakravarthy, Russin and O'Reilly ([NAACL-SRW 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-srw.1/), snippet). OTTER is the closest VLA. Its frozen-CLIP text-to-patch cosine matching is position-free in the keys, with absolute sin-cos PE in the values. It reaches **61% on LIBERO unseen vs OpenVLA 48%** and collapses to 15% when CLIP is fine-tuned ([OTTER](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03734), full text). The project's motivating measurement, that ViT patches linearly encode position (R^2 0.72/0.52 SigLIP, 0.89/0.85 DINOv2), is close to known probing results. "What DINO saw" probes positional ramps in DINO features ([2603.16840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16840), snippet). Mannes et al. show that permuting APE features costs **24–31 top-1 points**, so the position is *used* ([2610.13992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13992), abstract). The behavioural layout shortcut is also documented. LIBERO-Plus Finding 5 calls it "positional bias", with UniVLA falling **95.2 → 34.3** under layout change ([LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626v1), full text). Nobody links the two causally in VLA encoders. B1 → B1.1 is that causal ablation: projecting out linear position removed the location memory (docs/PLAN_BINDER.md §10, internal). Verdict on (3): **partial**. The recipe is known; the measured motivation and its causal test are new.

**The role-slot bottleneck** collides with Pelican-VLA 0.5. Pelican masks all action→prefix attention so that actions read the scene only through 32 generic BotTokens, under flow matching with "no object labels, segmentation masks, or attention supervision". Its target-attention IoU rises 0.054 → 0.124, but it reports no success-rate ablation ([Pelican-VLA 0.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06655), full text). Verdict: **partial**. A role-indexed bottleneck carrying object-frame pose is unclaimed, but "a narrow channel makes attention focus" is the obvious rival explanation.

**V-REx across layouts** had no VLA hit. The nearest prior is IRMv1 on a GAIL discriminator in MuJoCo, with mixed results and image domains left as future work ([2308.09189](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.09189v1), full text), plus ICIL on control and healthcare tasks ([2311.01489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01489), abstract). The 2026 VLA robustness papers use other losses and leave layout out. RoVLA uses paraphrase, flow-timestep and adversarial consistency losses. It reaches 73.0 on LIBERO-Plus Layout and concedes that pi0.5 is better on Layout ([RoVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19678v1), full text). DILL's limitations section **names object layouts as an uncovered shortcut** ([DILL](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37165v1), partial full text). Verdict: **novel application, not a new method**. The objections are strong. With few, similar environments, IRM/REx-type objectives can fail to remove spurious features (Rosenfeld et al. 2010.05761, prior knowledge), and penalty relaxations conflict with ERM ([PAIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07766), abstract). Tuned ERM matches them (DomainBed, prior knowledge). LIBERO-Plus already gains **68.7 → 77.6** on layout from mixed fine-tuning alone ([LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626v1)). There is also a flow-matching-specific confound that no paper addresses. Per-environment loss is dominated by the noise level and the action variance, so raw variance penalises intrinsically harder layouts. τ sampling must be matched, or the loss normalised per environment.

The integrated objection to BindVLA is "RoboGround's role queries plus Pelican's bottleneck plus an off-the-shelf invariance loss, with an oracle role parse that LIBERO templates supply for free". The answers are a noisy or learned role parser with a free-form paraphrase test, matched-capacity rivals, and per-component ablations that fail in the direction the theory predicts on the split it predicts.

## Recommended positioning, baselines and open verifications

The paper should lead with **measurements**. The first referring-depth and compound-divergence grading of binding across five flow VLAs and their published fixes. The LIBERO mirror as a caveat that changes how zero-shot grounding numbers on that benchmark are read. The positional-shortcut chain from linearly decodable ViT position, to location-memory binding, to its removal by nullspace projection, framed as the first *causal* link between known encoder probing results and the documented LIBERO-Plus positional bias. The theory should be claimed as **one falsifiable account plus one criterion**. The account: binding, not operators, is where systematicity fails, with ReGuide's factorisation used to predict unseen-compound success before rollout. The criterion: identifiability requires a transfer-scored selection rule, of which leave-layout-out prequential MDL is the instance. The B1.2 collapse (0.860 → 0.300) is its motivating demonstration. Invariance, identifiability, multiplicativity and the shape bias should appear as *connections to* IRM/ICP, Wiedemer et al., Okawa et al. and Kemp et al., each cited by name. BindVLA should be sold as the architecture that implements the account, judged by whether each component's ablation fails where the theory says it will. The paper should not claim "a new invariance method", "first unsupervised grounding", "first role slots", "first bottleneck VLA" or "first proof of compositional generalisation". It should not claim the CD contrast or atom invariance as confirmed, because the project's current pre-registered data contradict both. Title framings such as "Learning to Bind by Transfer, Not by Fit" fit the defensible ground. "How Invariance Fixes It" overclaims until a BindVLA closed-loop result exists.

## Conclusion

The novelty map and the project's own pre-registered results point the same way. The defensible contributions are the axes the field has not measured and the *criterion* that explains why fit-based learning finds shortcuts. Neither the invariance theorem nor the BindVLA modules qualify. The B1 → B1.1 → B1.2 sequence is more valuable than it looks, because it is a clean three-step demonstration of the identifiability argument. Position in the features produced location memory. Removing it left types bound but relations missing. An identification-scored MDL then pushed nouns into layout codes and lost transfer. That sequence is a better advertisement for leave-layout-out selection than any theorem restated from IRM.

The main risk is not a collision. It is that the theory's two sharpest predictions, flat factorised error in CD and atom invariance, currently fail on the project's own units, and CD is confounded with task there. Before BindVLA results are framed as confirming the theory, the project needs a CD split varied within task families. It also needs one demonstration that a context-ordered criterion assigns nouns to appearance and lateral words to relative geometry. Without those two, the honest paper is an atlas plus a refuted-and-revised account. That is still publishable, but it is not the paper the current titles promise.
