# A role-addressed structure for VLAs: proposal and vetting record (draft, 2026-11-12)

This document joins the failure account (docs/ARCH_FAILURES.md) to the structural fixes tested so far. Every claim cites a registered read. Sections marked PENDING wait on reads that are queued.

**Summary (2026-11-14).**

*The failures.* Current VLAs fail in four ways that are rooted in the architecture. Each failure here meets criteria C1 and C2: the needed information is in the input, and the failure persists across models.
- **F1, the held object picks the destination.** It appears on 4 of 5 models and in two scenes. In the second scene it is total: 0 of 720 episodes.
- **F1m, the held object's outline picks the motor program.**
- **F7, relational language is ignored.**
- **F6, a target seen once and then hidden leaves almost no trace.**

*The fix and how it compares.* An input-only front end (role sequencer, role views, a held-object box, role phrases) works on frozen pi0.5:
- it lifts new pairings from .454 to .938;
- it has no cost on trained tasks;
- it is pre-registered as EQUIVALENT to a reimplemented ReGuide (SR §55).

On frozen GR00T the views help (+.204, SR §56), but the held-object box adds nothing and costs .07 on trained tasks. In scene 2, on frozen pi0.5, handing over only at a hold of the named mug fixes part of the new pairings at no cost to the trained tasks (.000 → .442, trained 1.00, FIX WORKS by the registered rule, SR §62). That gain comes from the named-mug trigger. SR §61's majority-rule router adds nothing over it on the default layout (G − H +.008). It also holds without simulator state: with OWLv2 boxes plus rendered depth and calibration in place of the simulator's mug identity and plate position, pi0.5 reaches .450 with trained tasks unchanged, and the cost of perception is −.021 [−.067, +.025] (FIX WORKS, SR §63). The same trigger reads FIX WORKS on pi0 (.267), and on GR00T, which is below the testability floor (.242). Every result is still below §54's .50 bar, and depth and calibration remain privileged. GR00T's high carry is unchanged by the box or a grip-width state swap (UNRESOLVED both), so F1m's cue is model-specific.

**The common mechanism (SR §50-§57).** The place is routed by whichever visual cue covaried with the destination in training, and the instruction does not select it. The cue differs by setting:
- in scene 1 on pi0.5, the held object's identity (F1) or its outline (F1m);
- in scene 2, the held mug's start position.

The scene-2 evidence (SR §57, m3_prereg s2_cue):
- When the two mugs' starting positions are swapped, pi0.5 places every carried mug on the plate on its starting side: 287 of 287, 0 on its trained plate.
- The new pairings then succeed (.95-1.00), and the trained halves drop to 0.
- pi0.5 reads no state, so the cue is in the images.

An input edit can hide a cue the task does not need, such as identity or outline. Position is a different case, because the grasp needs it, so in scene 2 views cannot remove the cue, and only a hand-over (SR §54) does. Position routing replicates on pi0 (.993 of 145 placements on the start side, identity RULED OUT), and with both mugs started on the plates' bisector pi0.5 falls back to identity (C .933, C′ .787; depth and first-carry step RULED OUT), so the cue is a ranking, position over identity, not a single cue, and with the plates moved apart from the start sides, pi0.5 places on the nearer plate, not the start-side one (0 of 241 disagreeing placements on the start-side plate; pi0 points the same way but is UNRESOLVED). Separating the two in a third layout, where the nearer plate is the smaller, less central one, pi0.5 follows neither alone. It takes the small nearer plate when that plate is the mug's trained one (158 of 158) and the large far plate when that plate is (.859 of 149). The registered label is IDENTITY. A pre-registered test on new states and new layouts confirms the majority rule: the place goes to the plate backed by two of {nearer, salient, trained plate}. On pi0.5 arm I, .912 [.883, .942] of 445 two-against-one placements go there: trained plate outvoted 153 of 153, salience outvoted .974, nearness outvoted .752. The registered label is MAJORITY CONFIRMED, and §59-§60's layouts replicate at .970. This holds on pi0.5 only, the nearness-outvoted cell is just above its .70 bar, and the reference planner's C1 is unmet on A2, Sx and Sm (SR §61; GR00T matches §59's nearer-plate routing but is below its testability floor, SR §60). Either way, the destination comes from scene layout and the mug's trained plate, not from the language (SR §58-§60).

*Where the fix runs out.* The views do not carry to a second scene (SR §50). There, only the hand-over to a target-only place removes F1 (SR §54). The contribution is therefore the diagnosis, meaning which cue routes each failure, more than the fix. See docs/NOVELTY.md.

## 2. The proposal

A VLA keeps its perception and its motor prior. Five structural parts route information between them, and none of them adds information.

1. **Role sequencer.** A proprioceptive hold event splits each task into an object phase and a target phase.
2. **Role phrases.** The prompt names only the active role.
3. **Role views.** Every non-active movable object is painted as a silhouette in both cameras, from a learned segmenter. Identity is hidden; shape and geometry stay.
4. **Referent resolver.** A relation in the instruction is resolved to one entity mask before the policy sees it; the other candidate is painted as a role view. Vetted on 2 relation families (SR §45.2). It covers fixed templates only and reads a parsed scene, so it is a routing fix, not a language-understanding result.
5. **Entity memory slot.** The segmenter's mask of the target and its depth-projected position are kept from the last frame where the target was seen. While it is hidden, the slot repaints it into both cameras at its remembered position, with occlusion by the gripper. Vetted on one occluder task (SR §45.6).

The same principles in their strongest form give the **hand-over variant**: the VLA acts until the hold event, then an object-blind parts policy places, reading learned perception.

## 4. What the vetting does not yet support

- **Resolved for pi0.5 by the frozen front end.** The retrained R2 (.425) trails frozen pi0.5 (.50), but the same structure applied at test time to frozen pi0.5 (role sequencer + role views + role phrases, no training) beats intact pi0.5 on new pairings, confirmed on fresh states (+.367, SR §47) at no trained-task cost. On GR00T, role views alone gain +.233 on main states (TV WINS, §46) and +.186 [+.033, +.361] on 60 new fresh states (TV WINS, §49) at no trained-task cost; the phrases hurt GR00T, so its front end is views only. The gain sits in N2, N3 and N5. N4 and N6 stay near 0 on GR00T and near intact on pi0.5 under every arm: the front end fires and paints correctly there (IoU .97-.99), but the held bottle still selects K4's high carry (F1m, SR §51). In the target phase the role views already paint the held bottle as a silhouette (tv_frozen's rule), so the carry is selected without the bottle's colour and texture: from its shape, the gripper's opening, or the arm's state. Hiding identity does not reach this route. **The cue is the held object's outline (SR §53, m3_prereg f1m_cue):** painting the held bottle as its bounding box in the target phase lifts N4+N6 from .625 to .812 (+.188 [+.075, +.325], CUE FOUND), cuts the high carry from .225 to .062, and costs nothing on trained tasks (K4 still carries high and succeeds .90-.95). Proprioception is ruled out by construction: the served pi05_libero takes no state input (state swaps change actions by exactly 0). This refines part 3: show shape for the grasp, hide the held object's shape for the carry (a hypothesis on one object; it does not contradict R2b, which boxed non-active objects in both phases). GR00T's gain is half of pi0.5's, and its cross-chunk excess falls only from +.44 to +.25. **On GR00T the refinement does not carry over (SR §56, m3_prereg groot_box, 40 new states).** The box lowers GR00T's N4+N6 high carry only from .92 to .82, and N4 / N6 stay at .075 / 0 (tvb − tv +.025 [−.025, +.100], UNRESOLVED). It also costs −.070 on trained tasks, because K4's own carry fails when it is lowered (K4 .825), so a GR00T front end should keep the held object's outline. GR00T does read state, but swapping in the cheese finger qpos changes nothing measurable: tvg − tv on N4+N6 is +.025 [−.025, +.100], UNRESOLVED, and on N4 / N6 the swap is only about .003 per finger. On K4, where the swap is large, K4 still carries high in 40 of 40 episodes and succeeds 1.00. Grip width is therefore not GR00T's carry cue either. Its carry is overdetermined by inputs this front end does not remove.
- **Separation from AC-VLA-style masking holds only at test time on pi0.5.** With matched phrases, silhouettes beat whole-wrist grey by +.442 (SR §48), and wrist grey with phrases also breaks the hold detector (it fires more than once in 65% of episodes). The retrained comparison R2 − R2w is still NO CLEAR DIFFERENCE, and the AC-VLA proxy is not its trained method. On GR00T without phrases, views beat wrist grey by +.236 but the lower bound misses 0 by .017 (NOT SEPARATED, SR §49); wrist grey there costs .34 on trained tasks, views .02.
- **Most fixes have prior art; the diagnosis and two narrow tests are new.** The hand-over variant and the frozen test-time front end overlap ReGuide, LiLo-VLA, Harness VLA and AC-VLA (no priority claim), while the stage-resolved five-model atlas, silhouettes against whole-wrist grey at a matched prompt on pi0.5, and the frozen-VLA memory slot have no direct prior; see docs/NOVELTY.md.
- **Everything rests on one scene,** with one seed per retrained rung. The failure atlas replicates on 20 fresh states (F1 on 4 of 4 models, F2 on 2 of 2; ARCH_FAILURES "Fresh-state replication"). The frozen front end is confirmed on fresh states (§47, §49); the retrained rungs (R0-R2, HP) are not re-run. **The second scene splits the story (SR §50):** the failure replicates at full strength (0 of 720; cross-chunk excess +1.00 on pi0.5), and the fix does not replicate (role views 0 on both models). There, two mugs share one shape, so hiding identity leaves only position and the trained two-step sequence, and the policies rebuild the trained end arrangement. Identity-hiding views fix F1 only where identity is the cue that routes the place; where the cue is the scene's trained arrangement, no test-time view tested here reaches it. That makes the failure deeper than the fix, which supports the failure account and limits the proposal. In scene 2 (where the role views already paint the non-target plate in the target phase), only the hand-over removes F1 (SR §54: cross-chunk excess +1.00 → +.008), so the part that generalises across scenes is the role sequencer plus a target-only place, which has prior art and here reads privileged positions.
- **Our refined frozen front end matches ReGuide and does not beat it (SR §52, §55; m3_prereg reguide, tvpb_rg).** ReGuide was reimplemented from its paper on frozen pi0.5, with our segmenter for poses. In §52, TV+P trailed it with no clear difference (−.067 [−.217, +.042]), and ReGuide was ahead on N4 / N6 because it takes the carry away from the policy. §55 added the held-object box (SR §53) to make tvpb and ran a pre-registered head-to-head on 40 new states. tvpb reached .938 and RG-perc .929 (RG-oracle .933, I .454). tvpb − RG-perc was +.008 [−.083, +.092]: EQUIVALENT within ±.10. tvpb − I was +.483, FRONT END HELPS. The box replicated on N4 + N6: +.263 [+.100, +.450], BOX ADDS. Neither system had a trained-task cost. ReGuide stays ahead on N6 (.95 against .80). Our reimplementation omits ReGuide's post-hand-back escort, so it may understate ReGuide. As a fix, the input-only front end equals prior work on one scene. Its contribution is diagnostic: the silhouettes-vs-wrist-grey, held-object-outline and memory-slot tests isolate which cue routes each failure.
- **F7R fixes only where the policy can act on a named referent.** On pi0, X-VLA and SmolVLA it is FIXED on 0 of 6 cells (SR §45.3): the resolver is exact (240/240), but the models fail the named plate bowl outright (0/20 each), so the front end reaches each model's own ceiling and stops. Two of the four parts (role views, referent resolver) remove routing failures; neither removes a scene habit the policy holds when the referent is named. Painting every distractor, the proposal's own role-view rule, does not lift that ceiling either (0 of 7 cells; SR §45.4): the policies stop grasping the cheese and grasp nothing instead. The referent resolver covers fixed templates only.
- **The memory slot is vetted on one task and is partial on GR00T.** M still trails the never-covered control d (pi0.5 −.125, GR00T −.46). The plate leaks back into view more often under M (45-49% of later queries) than under b (34-43%), so late views may carry part of the gain. The occluder rule changed twice after policy-free checks, before any rollout.
- **New layouts are unsolved by every system.**
