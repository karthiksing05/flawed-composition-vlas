# The binder survives novelty checks; its parts do not

As of 27 October 2026, PLAN_BINDER is novel as a combination and as a set of measurements, but almost none of its individual ingredients is new, and the paper has to be written that way. **(A) The failure atlas is the strongest claim.** Nobody has stratified VLA binding error by referring-expression depth (type < instance < relational sub-region) or by compound divergence, and nobody has shown that the existing fixes degrade along those axes. The headline "VLAs move right but bind wrong" is crowded, and it must be cited, not claimed. **(B) The theory is partial.** The factorisation success = P(binding) x competence is ReGuide's Eq. 1. The union bound is textbook. Conformal sets with ask-for-help are KnowNo. What is new is the falsifiable holistic-vs-factorised contrast in compound divergence, the measured atom-invariance test, and role-wise allocation of the conformal alpha. **(C) The solution is novel as a system.** No paper attaches a few-KB, annotation-free, contact-supervised lexicon to a frozen flow VLA through an adapter that writes deep image-token K/V. But region discovery (Amir et al., CAST), per-channel word models with unsupervised channel assignment (Taniguchi et al. 2017), product-of-experts AND-composition (G3, Matuszek) and the grasp/release signal itself (SPARC, Robo2VLM) are all prior art. Only the parent-relative "A of B" recursion and the frozen-VLA plug-in lack a precedent. **(D) The base-VLM measurement is open.** No paper measures referring-expression grounding before and after robot fine-tuning. The crutch remedy is only partial, because frozen-generalist copies (TwinBrainVLA) and external detectors (ReGuide, VAP) are standard. The paper should therefore lead with the atlas and the base-VLM measurement, present the theory as a falsifiable account rather than as deep mathematics, and sell the binder on what it can do that a zero-shot pointer cannot: part words, in-domain names and zero annotation. The evidence base is uneven. A few papers were read in full HTML through a summarising fetch tool, many only through abstracts or search snippets, and ICLR 2027 OpenReview submissions were not searched, so every "novel" verdict below is provisional.

## Claim A: the depth gradient is unclaimed, the headline is not

### Six 2026 papers already own "skills robust, grounding brittle"

The qualitative claim that flow VLAs keep their motor skill and bind the instruction to the wrong referent is now crowded. "Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding" finds that for SmolVLA and pi0.5 "manipulation skill acquisition is decoupled from instruction following" ([arXiv 2602.24143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24143)). **ReGuide reports bare-policy composition averages of 8.5 (pi0), 39.0 (pi0.5), 20.6 (X-VLA) and 13.0 (GR00T N1.7)** across 16 one-factor composition cells. It writes success as **Pr(S_k) = beta_k x gamma_k**, binding times competence ([ReGuide HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). "When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories" finds that **69% of 381 pi0.5 probes follow a familiar behaviour**, and that a prefix-KV swap redirects the arm in **109/200 rollouts versus 0/200 for controls** ([arXiv 2609.39971](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39971)). VLA-Trace ([arXiv 2605.30117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30117)), the goal-selection/motor separation of 2603.19233 ([arXiv 2603.19233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19233)) and RoboSemanticBench's wrong-target selections ([arXiv 2606.02277](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02277)) complete the picture. Failure taxonomies that split grounding from execution also exist: ProGAL-VLA ([arXiv 2604.09824](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09824)), RoboSPA for pi0.5 and X-VLA ([arXiv 2609.05324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05324)), and "What Can One Sentence Fix?", which finds task misunderstanding in **47% (OFT) and 50% (pi0.5)** of 133 failing LIBERO-90 pairs ([arXiv 2608.29967](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29967)).

### No paper grades error by referring depth or by compound divergence

What none of these does is order binding error by referring-expression depth, or sweep compound divergence at fixed atom divergence. The closest neighbours each fall short:

No robotics paper found uses Keysers et al.'s compound-divergence metric ([arXiv 1912.09713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.09713)). The fixes have never been stratified either. ReGuide, ECT, CRAFT ([arXiv 2610.00524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00524)), Pointing-VLA ([arXiv 2608.23138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23138)) and CAG ([arXiv 2602.17659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17659)) report no per-depth results. The claim that the fixes also fail at depth must therefore be shown experimentally. It cannot be cited.

**Verdict on A: novel for the depth and compound-divergence axes; prior art for the binding-versus-motor dissociation.** Covering all five VLAs is broader than any single paper (ReGuide covers pi0, pi0.5, X-VLA and GR00T N1.7, but not SmolVLA or N1.5), but breadth alone is not a contribution.

### The objection: "relabelled LIBERO perturbations, and the depth is a difficulty confound"

The strongest objection is that the atlas re-describes known LIBERO-PRO/Plus-style brittleness, and that "depth" is confounded with visual difficulty. Sub-region targets such as drawers are smaller, are articulated and appear less often in training, so the gradient could reflect perception or frequency rather than compositional depth. Three experiments answer it. First, hold referent pixel area, training frequency and entity count fixed while varying depth. That also sets the result against Campbell et al.'s interference account, which predicts error rising with the number of similar entities ([arXiv 2411.00238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00238)). Second, report beta and gamma separately per depth and per backbone, crediting ReGuide's factorisation, so the gradient is shown to sit in beta and not in gamma. Third, run ReGuide with its perception-grounded variant on sub-region units. ReGuide grounds through GroundingDINO entity boxes, and its perception variant is already 7.0 points below oracle grounding ([ReGuide HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). A pose-based wrapper that has no pose for "top drawer" is the cleanest demonstration that a fix degrades with depth. One competing result needs care: a 2026 Transformer study reports "the more compound divergence the better" in its setting ([arXiv 2609.13144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13144), snippet only). The CD sweep must show its direction for each model and not assume it.

## Claim B: three theorems, one with real content

### The factorisation and the bound are borrowed; the contrast is not

**T1/T4 (success = P(binding) x competence).** This is ReGuide's beta x gamma, and it is the chain rule whenever success requires the correct binding. Okawa et al. already show multiplicative reliance on constituent performance ([arXiv 2310.09336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09336)). The novel content is the testable claim that competence given a correct binding does not depend on the compound, and the use of separately measured rates to *predict* success on unseen compounds. No paper found does either.

**T2 (union bound under atom invariance).** Bounding P(any atom wrong) by the sum of per-atom errors is a textbook union bound. Wiedemer et al. prove exact identification on unseen slot combinations under compositional support, and list a bounded-error version as future work ([arXiv 2307.05596](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05596)). Ram et al. give a Rademacher-style gap bound with no modular-versus-holistic contrast ([arXiv 2405.02350](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02350)). Uselis et al. give necessity results for linear, orthogonal concept representations ([arXiv 2602.24264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24264)). Lippl and Stachenfeld show kernel models compute "conjunction-wise additive" functions, with "memorization leak" ([arXiv 2405.16391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16391)). That is the closest formal treatment of exemplar learners, but it is not indexed by divergence. Prop. 4.1 of 2607.29687 bounds the change in loss by marginal plus instruction-compositional plus context-action shift, for *any* model, so it cannot separate factorised from holistic binders ([arXiv 2607.29687](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29687)). Two contributions survive, both small but real: an error-level, non-asymptotic relaxation of Wiedemer's logic applied to role binding in VLAs, and a lower bound for nearest-neighbour binders that grows with compound divergence. No proof of that lower bound was found anywhere.

**T3 (split-conformal binding sets with abstention).** This is close to prior art. KnowNo builds conformal sets over "pick X, place Y" options and asks for help when more than one survives ([PMLR v229](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)). IntroPlan tightens such sets over intents ([NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8451a20c5a7e0ee5671dda28f7daf7f3-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Conformal grounding wrappers exist for video moments ([arXiv 2608.07434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07434)), and conformal detection with Bonferroni over coordinates is established ([PMLR v204](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v204/andeol23a/andeol23a.pdf)). Not found anywhere: role-factorised sets inside a VLA's binder (an object set crossed with a region set, joint coverage >= 1 - sum of alpha_r), with abstention reported separately from motor failure.

**Verdicts: T1/T4 partial (prior-art factorisation, novel prediction test); T2 partial (trivial bound, novel contrast and lower bound); T3 partial, leaning prior art.**

### The objection: "atom invariance is the whole theorem, and it is false for roles"

The strongest objection to T2 is that it is trivial and its assumption untestable on unseen compounds. Once a primitive's error is independent of its context, CD-independence follows by definition. In "put A on B", a role swap is also an error that no atom-wise bound sees unless roles are atoms. A second objection is that the bound is vacuous at depth: four atoms at 15% each give 0.6. The plan's claim that invariance "holds by construction" in the product of experts is weaker than it sounds (our inference). The experts share one region-proposal step and one parent region, so an error in clustering or in resolving B propagates to every atom inside it. Construction makes the *scorer* factorised, not the *errors* independent. Four moves answer this. First, treat role assignment and parent resolution as atoms with their own epsilon. Second, test invariance directly by measuring epsilon(a) conditioned on co-occurring atoms, with the CI of its slope in CD reported. Third, prove, or at least construct, the nearest-neighbour lower bound, so the theorem's content becomes the *gap* between the two binder classes. Fourth, report the empirical gap between the union bound and observed error at each depth. For T3, the answer to "KnowNo with a new score head" is to tie the role-wise alpha split to the T2 sum. Coverage on unseen compounds then holds only when invariance holds, which turns the conformal set into a test of the assumption. Abstention cost must be reported honestly: one conformal hallucination filter needed more than 80% abstention to keep hallucination below 5% ([papers.cool 2606.16667](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2606.16667)). For T4, the product of three separately measured terms needs its own independence assumption, stated as such. The theory reads best framed as turning Fodor and Pylyshyn's systematicity argument into a falsifiable quantitative statement, not as new mathematics.

GSR, the feared "same idea" collision of August 2026, turned out on a full read to be about robustness to paraphrase. It re-encodes the instruction with a frozen T5-large, injects it as sidecar K/V into action blocks, and retrains the action head. It has no referent binding, no depth axis, no theory and no image-token masks ([arXiv 2608.02497](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02497)).

### The adapter's neighbours edit the input, the whole cache, or the action blocks

Mask and visual-prompt conditioning is crowded, but nearly all of it works at the pixels or retrains the policy. VAP tints the target's SAM2 mask and lifts WidowX shaver picks from **0% to 71.3%** and real pi0.5 selection from **30% to 80%**. Its learned soft-prompt token gives only marginal gains, a useful negative datapoint for token-level conditioning ([arXiv 2512.20014](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20014)). Internal edits on frozen policies differ from the plan in kind. The 2609.39971 swap replaces the *whole* prefix cache (image, language and state together), tests only the final layer against all layers, and drives source-task completion from 200/200 to 0 ([arXiv 2609.39971](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39971)). SPECTRA-VLA adds a mask-weighted residual to post-projector visual tokens of frozen OpenVLA, but not to K/V or deep layers, and reports no closed-loop success ([arXiv 2610.19651](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19651v1)). TwinBrainVLA feeds stop-gradient K/V from a frozen VLM into joint attention, without role masks ([arXiv 2601.14133](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14133)). IGAR recalibrates attention toward instruction tokens without training ([arXiv 2603.06001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06001)). ECT's counterfactual pairs keep the instruction and change the scene, and it fine-tunes the policy. The plan's same-scene, different-target pairs training a frozen-policy adapter have no precedent found.

**Verdict on C: novel as a combination; components mostly prior art; the adapter is partial (open mechanism, crowded neighbourhood).**

### The objection: "2010-era grounded word learning on templated LIBERO, beaten by Grounded-SAM"

The strongest objection runs as follows. This is Roy 2002, Taniguchi 2017 and G3 with DINOv2 features plus SPARC's gripper-phase trick. Cross-situational learning is trivial on LIBERO's templated language. A zero-shot pointer (Grounded-SAM, RoboPoint, MOKA) is a stronger binder. And the adapter is a mask-conditioned version of a KV swap already shown in 2609.39971, with the binding supplied externally. The answers have to be experimental. On the binder, show it working with paraphrased, non-templated language and distractor-heavy scenes, and on part words open-vocabulary detectors miss. Report words learned per demo and ambiguity curves (distractors, synonym rate). Report per-word confounds where words always co-occur in training, as the plan already intends. Run head-to-heads against Grounded-SAM, RoboPoint and a SPARC-style detector pipeline, and ablate MDL against Taniguchi-style Bayesian channel assignment. Add a part-quality check, because Choudhury et al. argue that clustering pre-computed features rarely finds meaningful parts ([NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/ec8ce6abb3e952a85b8551ba726a1227-Paper.pdf)) and a 2025 study calls single-image DINO patch clustering "highly unreliable" ([arXiv 2507.06928](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06928)). On the adapter, VAP tint overlays and RoboGround-style mask channels are mandatory baselines. The adapter has to win where an overlay cannot: sub-region and goal roles, multi-role scenes, no image artifacts. It also has to show that it changes WHERE while preserving HOW, which is precisely what the 2609.39971 swap failed to do (source completion fell to 0). A layer-range ablation (input token, final layer, deep image K/V) and transfer of one trained adapter to unseen bindings with no target demos complete the case.

## Claim D: nobody has measured what fine-tuning does to referring grounding

### Forgetting is documented on VQA and POPE, never on referring expressions

Evidence that VLAs lose VLM abilities is plentiful but measured on other axes. **TwinBrainVLA reports Qwen3-VL's POPE falling from 88.87% to 0.04%** after VLA training, with no RefCOCO or pointing evaluation ([arXiv 2601.14133](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14133)). "Don't Blind Your VLA" compares OpenVLA-7B with its Prismatic base on board-selection tasks (Arrow 0.26 vs 0.47, Public-info 0.24 vs 0.62) and a linear probe (77.48% vs 79.88%). It has no localization metric and does not test pi0 ([arXiv 2510.25616](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25616)). Knowledge Insulation states that action-expert gradients damage the VLM, and co-trains pi0.5 on bounding-box localization data, but reports no before/after VLM benchmark ([arXiv 2505.23705](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23705)). StarVLA reports weaker object grounding after action-only fine-tuning, but only in a search snippet with unverified numbers ([arXiv 2604.05014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05014)). A counterpoint argues that forgetting is easier to avoid than assumed ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf/278c682d3fdae858e47cc6e1111e5d282dd787fa.pdf)). On the base side, PaliGemma has 1024 location and 128 segmentation tokens ([arXiv 2407.07726](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07726v1)). Its published RefCOCO numbers are **single-task transfer** results, for example 73.40 mIoU on RefCOCO val at pt-224. **No mix-checkpoint RefCOCO number is published** ([big_vision README](https://github.com/google-research/big_vision/blob/main/big_vision/configs/proj/paligemma/README.md); [model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/paligemma/model-card)). No part-level referring evaluation of PaliGemma was found.

**Verdict on D: the measurement is novel; the crutch binder is partial.** TwinBrainVLA keeps a frozen generalist VLM inside the policy. ReGuide, VAP and Embodied-R1 ([arXiv 2508.13998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13998)) use external grounders.

### The objection: "a VLM detector producing masks is engineering, and the 'base' is not the policy's base"

A reviewer will say that calling PaliGemma "the policy's own backbone" adds nothing unless two things are shown: the fine-tuned backbone lost exactly this ability, and the base grounds better than an off-the-shelf detector. A second trap is lineage. pi0 starts from PaliGemma pt-224 (prior knowledge, unverified), not from mix, and pt-224 without transfer fine-tuning is weaker than the published ~73 mIoU. If B0 uses a mix checkpoint, the reviewer will call it an external detector. A third objection is that pi0.5's KI recipe co-trains on localization, so Physical Intelligence could argue that grounding is preserved. The answer is a matched-protocol measurement (`detect X` / `segment X`) on four backbones: PaliGemma pt-224, PaliGemma mix, the pi05_base prefix and the released fine-tuned openpi checkpoints. It should be stratified by the same depth levels as the atlas and set against GroundingDINO, which is the detector ReGuide uses. If the base grounds depth 0–1 but fails at sub-regions, D becomes a clean "fine-tuning lost type and instance grounding, and nobody ever had sub-regions" result. That outcome motivates C, so B0's likely partial failure strengthens the paper.

The recommended positioning orders the contributions by how defensible they are. **First**, the atlas is a *measurement* contribution: the first referring-depth and compound-divergence grading of binding across five flow VLAs and six fixes, on a split held out at the weights. It explicitly credits ReGuide's beta x gamma and 2609.39971's retrieval finding, and claims to decompose them along new axes. **Second**, the base-VLM measurement (D) is the paper's second empirical finding, framed as *where the binding ability goes*: present at pretraining for type and instance, lost in fine-tuning, absent at sub-regions throughout. **Third**, the theory is a falsifiable account whose one non-trivial prediction is the divergence between the binder classes: holistic error rises with CD, factorised error stays flat. The union bound and conformal sets are the tools that make that prediction checkable and deployable, not the claims themselves. **Fourth**, the binder plus adapter is the "existence proof" that a factorised binder can be built without annotation and attached without touching the policy. It is sold on parts, in-domain names, few KB and zero labels, with each borrowed component cited by name. A title-level framing such as "binding at depth is where VLA systematicity fails" keeps the paper on its open ground. Words like "first unsupervised grounding" or "first conformal grounding" invite the Taniguchi and KnowNo rebuttals.

## Conclusion

The novelty check moves the paper's centre of gravity. The plan treats the binder as the main event and the atlas as motivation. The prior-work map says the opposite. Every *component* of the binder has a named ancestor that a reviewer will reach for (Taniguchi 2017, SPARC, CAST, G3, KnowNo, the 2609.39971 swap). The two *measurements*, depth-graded binding across models and fixes and referring-grounding loss from PaliGemma to pi0.5, have no ancestor at all. The theory's real asset is a single falsifiable divergence: holistic error rises with compound divergence and factorised error stays flat. It is worth making that the one plot every reader remembers, and proving the nearest-neighbour lower bound that turns the trivial union bound into a two-sided result.

Two experiments carry the most weight for novelty and are cheap. B0 run on four backbones at three depths will either establish D outright or show that sub-region grounding never existed, and either outcome motivates C. Perception-grounded ReGuide on sub-region units directly tests "fixes fail at depth" against the strongest published fix. The binder is worth building, but it should be judged on part words and paraphrase against Grounded-SAM and VAP, not on templated LIBERO phrases, where a reviewer will dismiss it as cross-situational counting.
